# Zero-player sims, tempo, chronicles, mood — findings for a self-playing colony sim

Scope: (a) zero-player/idle design and "just enough influence", (b) time compression and event-driven pacing, (c) event logs / chronicles, (d) mood/recreation and unproductive roles, (e) legibility in text interfaces. Every mechanic claim below carries a link. Sentences marked "(from memory, unverified)" have no source and should be treated as a hypothesis.

## 1. Per-game findings

### (a) Zero-player and idle: how much influence is enough

Progress Quest (2002) is the null case: the whole UI is a stat block plus four progress bars (Experience, Plot Development, Encumbrance, Quest), the loop is kill → fill encumbrance → sell everything → buy equipment → repeat, level N takes `(20 + 1.15^N) × 60` seconds, and plot acts advance on a timer with **no mechanical effect whatsoever** ([official tutorial](https://progressquest.com/ubuntu/tutorial.php), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Quest), [Alex Meub's breakdown](https://alexmeub.com/progress-quest-the-original-idle-game/)). The one design reading available calls it hypnotic rather than engaging — it "extends auto-attack to auto-everything" ([Kill Ten Rats](https://www.killtenrats.com/2013/01/05/heirs-to-progress-quest/)). Zero player→system channel, so zero retention design. No statements by Fredricksen himself could be found.

Godville is the only ZPG that actually designed an economy of influence, and it is the closest reference we have. Godpower is a 0-200% bar; the price list is Voice of God 5%, Encourage 25%, Punish 25%, Arena/Dungeon/Miracle 50%, Resurrect 0% ([GodWiki: Influence](https://web.archive.org/web/20251101222538/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Influence), [Godpower](https://web.archive.org/web/20260714174116/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Godpower)). A full bar therefore buys **2-4 interventions**. Almost every lever is probabilistic: Encourage typically restores ~60-180 HP but **can backfire**, healing the monster instead; the "Death" command is described as one of the least likely to be obeyed; voice commands land ~**30%** of the time in the Arena; "dig" succeeds 1/3 ([Encouragement](https://web.archive.org/web/20260311003548/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Encouragement), [Voice of god](https://web.archive.org/web/20260723222638/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Voice_of_god), [Arena](https://web.archive.org/web/20260311154324/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Arena)). The single deterministic action, Miracle (100%, +500 HP or +25% quest progress), is gated behind a temple that costs **1000 gold bricks**, one brick per 3000 coins spent on Encourage/Punish ([Miracle](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Miracle), [Temple](https://web.archive.org/web/20260811143649/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Temple)). Godville also enforces windows with **no influence at all**: the first 2 minutes of an arena fight, plus a "ZPG Arena" during the first 5 minutes of every hour. Voice of God is capped at **120 characters**. The diary holds exactly **10 entries** (20 after the Book of Creation), the hero writes every few minutes, and older lines are simply discarded — the observation window is a ~10-15 minute rolling feed, not an archive ([Diary](https://web.archive.org/web/20251115003428/https://wiki.godvillegame.com/Diary)). Business failure is documented by the developers themselves: donations did not cover server costs and monetisation was introduced within months purely to pay hosting ([Habr retrospective](https://habr.com/ru/articles/415407/)).

Universal Paperclips is the reference for **finite** self-playing design. Act transitions are hard gates: "Release the HypnoDrones" needs exactly 100 Trust and **zeroes all unspent Trust**, forcing allocation before the handoff; Space Exploration needs 120,000 ops, 10M MW-sec, 5 octillion clips and **zero remaining Earth resources** ([Fandom: Stages](https://universalpaperclips.fandom.com/wiki/Stages)). Trust arrives on Fibonacci clip thresholds (3k/5k/8k/13k, `1000 × (Fib(trust+5) − 5)`), capped at 66 ([Trust](https://universalpaperclips.fandom.com/wiki/Trust)). Crucially, as the main loop automates, the game **hands decisions back**: Strategic Modeling costs 1000 ops per strategy, runs (strategies)² rounds, and the optimum deliberately moves — Greedy pays 5,329 yomi normally but 20,814 after Theory of Mind + Strategic Attachment, so the solved decision un-solves itself ([Strategic Modeling](https://universalpaperclips.fandom.com/wiki/Strategic_Modeling)). Quantum Computing keeps a manual click meaningful to the end: value oscillates on a ~62.8 s cycle, a well-timed click yields up to ~2,750 ops, a bad one −10,000 ([Quantum Computing](https://universalpaperclips.fandom.com/wiki/Quantum_Computing)). Playtime is 6-10 h, not 4-6 (reports 5:47-8:52; speedrun 1:40:05, [speedrun.com/upc](https://www.speedrun.com/upc)). Lantz on finiteness: "I really wanted to kind of put the hooks in and then let people off the hook… gets in your head, takes you over and then lets you go" and "one of the things I'm most proud of is that the ending's good" ([hey.gg](https://www.hey.gg/blog/frank-lantz)).

WorldBox shows what a god-sandbox does without a goal. The sim genuinely runs itself — a village needs an 8×8 fully-land zone on an island of ≥300 connected land tiles, then auto-builds fireplace → town hall → 3-5 tier-1 houses before any resource gathering; wars start automatically on low inter-kingdom relations, rebellions on low village-to-kingdom relations, and villages are hard-capped by resources inside their own borders ([WorldBox wiki: Civilizations](https://worldbox-sandbox-god-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Civilizations,_Kingdoms,_and_Villages), [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206560/WorldBox__God_Simulator/)). The observed failure: with no goal, sessions collapse into using the destructive tools because destruction gives instant feedback, and reviewers report roughly four hours of interest ([summarised in Wikipedia: WorldBox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)).

The Sims is the best-documented autonomy system, and its numbers are public. Objects carry *advertisements* and "Sims (not under direct player control) choose what to do by selecting, from all of the possible behaviors in all of the objects, the behavior that maximizes their current happiness" ([Forbus & Wright, *Some notes on programming objects in The Sims*](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c95-gd/2001/Programming%20Objects%20in%20The%20Sims.pdf)). Release values from the Edith editor: distance attenuation preset "moderate" = **0.3**; **autonomy threshold = 60 per interaction** — free will is a per-action gate, not one global slider; a toilet advertises Bladder 0-70, a Joy Booth advertises Social 20-120 / Fun 20-100 / Mood 20-100. Their own documented tuning failure: "We've created a kind of SimHeroin… Early versions of the Joy Booth were so addictive that Sims would continue using it until they collapsed." Zubek's independent write-up of the same family of formulas names two more failure modes: distance attenuation producing "bird in hand" behaviour where an agent always takes a much worse nearby action, and **false advertisements creating action loops** where the agent re-picks an action that never pays ([Zubek, *Needs-Based AI*](https://zubek.net/robert/publications/Needs-based-AI-draft.pdf)). Zubek also states the arbitration rule directly: weighted-random over the top N is "a good compromise between having some predictability about what will happen, and not having the agent look unpleasantly deterministic". And on legibility: the player cannot see need values and "is likely to build an entirely different mental model of what the agent is doing", which is why thought bubbles exist; a Sims developer adds "if we used actual language, the game would flatten and shrink" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims)).

Idle-game math, from the genre's main designer source — Pecorella, *Quest for Progress*, GDC Europe 2016 ([slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf)): cost grows as `cost_base × growth^owned` while production grows linearly, which *is* the wall; AdVenture Capitalist uses growth 1.07 with x2 multipliers at 25 and 50 owned; prestige should reset at **+50%-200%** expected gain; and an **Active Skill on a 5-minute-to-several-hours cooldown "increases player agency, letting them burst income or get over a hump"**. He also names his own churn: Egg Inc's **2-hour offline-earnings cap** — "I think this was a mistake, I churned out myself largely because of this". Prestige formulas differ in whether they read lifetime or per-run earnings; per-run formulas (Egg Inc, Clicker Heroes) permit optimal strategies that never progress ([Math of Idle Games III](https://www.kongregate.com/en/pages/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)).

On "how much influence": Molyneux on Populous — "the idea that you **influence** the game as opposed to **controlling** the game" — and the terraforming that expressed it was originally a workaround for broken pathfinding ([Digital Antiquarian](https://www.filfre.net/2016/06/peter-molyneuxs-kingdom-in-a-box/)). Black & White is the case where more control made it worse: asked whether Black & White 2's direct-control sliders "ruined the creature as a person", Molyneux answered **"Agreed"**, adding "that idea wasn't balanced enough" ([GameSpot, archived](https://www.bwgame.net/threads/some-comments-from-peter-molyneux-about-bw2.2157/)). Sylvester's GDC 2017 slides put the thesis bluntly — "**Not a game — a story generator**", "Loss is an essential part of a story, not its conclusion… your mechanics must include loss and recovery", engagement via **apophenia** driven by "abstracted feedback" plus "long-term relevance", and "RimWorld has graphics like a novel has a typeface" ([slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Sylvester_Tynan_RimWorld_Contrarian_Ridiculous.pdf)). Crawford's *Process Intensity* supplies the formal axis: "crunch per bit", the ratio of computation to data — the axis a watching game wins on ([erasmatazz.com](https://www.erasmatazz.com/library/the-journal-of-computer/jcgd-volume-1/process-intensity.html)).

### (b) Time compression and event-driven pacing

Concrete tempo baselines. Dwarf Fortress fortress mode advances 72 in-game seconds per tick; 1 day = 1200 ticks, 1 year = 403,200 ticks ([DF wiki: Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)). At the default `[FPS_CAP:100]` in `init.txt` ([DF wiki: Init.txt](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Init.txt)) that is 4,032 s = **67 real minutes per game year**, and only if the CPU keeps up — late forts do not.

RimWorld: 60 ticks per real second at speed 1, 2,500 ticks per in-game hour, 60,000 ticks (16.67 min) per day, 60 days per year. Speed buttons are **x1 / x3 / x6**, and **x15 exists only in developer mode** ([RimWorld wiki: Time](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time)). So the fastest a normal player can run is ~2.8 real hours per game year. Our target of a game year in a few real minutes is ~30-60x faster than either shipped sim; nobody in this genre runs that fast with a per-citizen simulation, which is a load-bearing constraint, not a detail.

RimWorld also **forces speed back to 1x during hostile events** and exposes separate "pause on minor threat / major threat" options; players have repeatedly asked to disable the forced deceleration for trivial dangers, and the Smart Speed mod exists specifically to override it and to set a configurable "event speed multiplier" ([Steam: Still no 'disable forced 1x speed'](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/1750150188485136328/), [Smart Speed mod](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1504723424)). Lesson: auto-slow is right, but the *threshold* for what deserves a slowdown must be tunable or it becomes an irritant.

Aurora 4X is the closest shipped model to "run fast until something happens": the player picks a time increment, ticks an **Auto Turns** checkbox, and the game keeps running increments of that size until an interrupt event affecting the player is generated; interrupts end automated turns and **cannot be switched off**. Movement inside an increment is broken into sub-pulses, and an event detected during a sub-pulse terminates the increment early ([Aurora4x wiki: Time, Turns and Interrupts](https://aurora4x.fandom.com/wiki/Time,_Turns_and_Interrupts); the pentarch mirror <http://aurorawiki.pentarch.org/index.php?title=Time,_Turns_and_Interrupts> was returning HTTP 522 during this research).

Roguelike auto-explore is the best-documented "what counts as an event" configuration in any game. DCSS ships `explore_stop` as a list option with named conditions — `items, stairs, shops, altars, portals, branches, runed_doors, greedy_pickup_smart, greedy_visited_item_stack` — plus `explore_stop_pickup_ignore` regexes to suppress specific pickups, and `runrest_stop_message` / `runrest_ignore_message` regexes matched against full message text to force or suppress interrupts. Per-activity control exists too: `interrupt_<delay> -= <type>` over delay types (`travel, rest, run, memorise, multidrop, ...`) and interrupt types (`abyss_exit_spawned, ally_attacked, ancestor_hp, force, full_hp, full_mp, hp_loss, hit_monster, keypress, message, mimic, monster, monster_attack, sense_monster, teleport`) ([DCSS options guide](https://crawl.akrasiac.org/docs/options_guide.txt)). The design content: interrupts are a **named, enumerable, user-editable set**, not a hardcoded judgement call.

Kerbal Space Program's "warp to next maneuver node" stops **one minute before** the node rather than at it ([KSP forums](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/107407-warp-to-next-maneuver-node-improvements/)). The known failure: when the node is far in the future, auto-warp overshoots and brakes late, by more than two hours in reported cases ([MechJeb2 issue #402](https://github.com/MuMech/MechJeb2/issues/402)). Rule: decelerate *before* the event so the watcher sees it happen, and never compute the stop point from a prediction that the fast-forward itself invalidates.

Crusader Kings is the counter-example on configurability. CK2 let players set per-message-type delivery; **CK3 shipped without notification customization**, and the community filled the gap with mods (Configurable News Feed, Less Event Spam, auto-pause management) ([Paradox forum: Notification settings](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/notification-settings.1445086/), [Less Event Spam](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2750102888)). Removing the filter is a regression players notice immediately.

### (c) Event logs and chronicles

Dwarf Fortress is the canonical fully-configurable announcement system and the canonical noise problem at the same time. Announcements come in tiers: minor (a line in the feed), major (pauses and centres the camera, plays a distinct wooden tick), and game-changing (pauses, zooms, opens a framed message box, then repeats the line in the feed). Every announcement type is a row with four independent switches — **Popup, Pause, Recenter, Alert** — editable in Settings → Announcements or as tokens in `announcements.txt`, e.g. `[MIGRANT_ARRIVAL:A_D:D_D:P:R]` where `:P` pauses and `:R` recentres; `/init/announcements.txt` holds defaults and `/prefs/announcements.txt` the current set. Combat results live in a *separate* stream ("reports"), not in announcements. The wiki's own advice about turning on alerts for combat strikes and wounds is the failure warning: **"don't get ambitious and change all the combat events to Alert, you'll regret it!"** ([DF wiki: Announcements](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Announcements)).

CK2's Chronicle is the opposite architecture and the one that matches our brief: it is not a filter over the message feed but a **separately authored stream**, written by explicit `on_action` hooks on a small set of notable event types — births and deaths, wars and battles, marriages, title acquisitions and usurpations, conversions, crusades, imprisonments, and rare supernatural sightings ([CK2 wiki: Chronicle](https://ck2.paradoxwikis.com/Chronicle)). CK3 dropped the per-message-type customisation CK2 had, and the community's most-used fixes are notification mods ([Paradox forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/notification-settings.1445086/)).

RimWorld's History screen is a useful non-textual chronicle: above wealth/population graphs runs a ribbon of coloured circles, one per random event, **blue/white for positive-or-neutral, yellow for negative, red for attacks**, chronologically aligned with the graph and filterable to the last **300 / 100 / 30 days** ([RimWorld wiki: Menus](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Menus)). This is a chronicle at three time scales without a single sentence of prose.

RimWorld also feeds its event history back into the world as culture. Sculpture descriptions are generated from a grammar that mixes pre-written fragments with **actual past colony events**: killings, social fights, drunkenness, vomiting, landing in drop pods, an object crafted, artwork created, a high skill level reached, ore struck, escaping a roof collapse, a person captured, a person recruited, an animal trained, an animal hunted, surgery done, someone on fire, someone going psychotic, someone giving up ([RimWorld wiki: Art](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Art)). The log is not only read — it is engraved on objects that then sit in rooms and generate mood.

Caves of Qud deliberately avoided a full historical simulation: it generates historical actions largely at random and then **rationalizes motives after the fact**, running "sifting patterns" over the action sequence and rendering the result through replacement grammars ([Grinblat & Bucklew, *Generation of mythic biographies in Caves of Qud*](https://www.freeholdgames.com/papers/Generation_of_mythic_biographies_in_Cavesofqud.pdf); [GDC 2018 talk](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024990/Procedurally-Generating-History-in-Caves)). Five of the six Sultans are fully generated, one is fixed.

### (d) Mood, recreation, and unproductive roles

RimWorld mood, with numbers ([RimWorld wiki: Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood)). Mood is 0-100%. Base mood is set by difficulty: **42 / 42 / 37 / 32 / 27 / 22** from Peaceful to Losing-is-Fun. A "mood target" is computed instantly from the sum of active thoughts; the displayed mood bar chases it asymmetrically at **+12 per in-game hour rising, −8 per hour falling**, and freezes while the pawn sleeps. Break thresholds sit at **35% minor / 20% major / 5% extreme**. The asymmetry and the lag are the design: short-term shocks do not instantly break anyone, but sustained ones do.

RimWorld recreation is a need with explicit thresholds and anti-monotony pressure ([RimWorld wiki: Recreation](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Recreation)). Ten recreation types exist (solitary, social, cerebral, dexterity, reading, television, telescope, music, chemical, food). Thresholds: 85-100% → **+10 mood**, 70-85% → **+5**, 30-70% → nothing, 15-30% → **−5**, 0-15% → **−10**, 0% → **−20**. The need falls at **2.5%/hour**, slowing to 1.75%/h below 30% and 1%/h below 15% — 51.6 in-game hours from full to starved. Gain is **36%/hour base**, multiplied by the activity's rate and by the pawn's tolerance. Tolerance rises by **2/3 of the recreation gained**, and a pawn becomes *bored* of a type above **50%** tolerance and un-bored below **30%**; a bored pawn refuses to seek that type at all. Tolerance decays per day at a rate set by colony wealth — **18%/day at <15k wealth down to 7%/day above 308k** — with the game explicitly recommending **2 recreation types at low wealth rising to 6 at high wealth**. That is a clean formal statement of "success raises the cultural bar".

Dwarf Fortress separates *mood* from *capability*. Stress is two numbers: short-term (−100,000..+100,000, tracks the visible mood face) and long-term (−50,000..+120,000, statuses at **+25,000 stressed / +50,000 haggard / +100,000 harrowed**). Under ideal conditions long-term stress rises at most 20,160/year and falls at most 43,564/year, so both breakdown and recovery take **years** ([DF wiki: Stress](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Stress)). Separately, unmet *needs* damage **focus**, which is a straight work multiplier: focus statuses run Very focused (≥140%) → Badly distracted (≤60%), giving up to **±50% effective skill** ([DF wiki: Need](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need)). Needs have weights 1/2/5/10 and a satisfied need resets to 400; the rule of thumb is to satisfy a weight-10 need every 3 months, weight-5 every 6, weight-2 yearly, weight-1 every two years. Needs include socialising, drinking, praying, staying occupied, doing something creative, doing something exciting, learning, family, friends.

RimWorld rituals are the best worked example of unproductive labour that is load-bearing. Each ritual has a **Quality** percentage assembled from named contributions — e.g. dance party: participant count (10) **+30%**, started at lightball **+20%**, six active loudspeakers **+30%**, room impressiveness 120 **+20%** — and quality maps to an outcome distribution: Terrible = 1/(4+16Q), Boring = 3/(4+16Q), Fun = 3Q/(1+4Q), Unforgettable = Q/(1+4Q). Dance-party outcomes give **−3 / −1 / +8 / +16 mood**, and Fun-or-better carries a **90% chance of +20% global work speed for 24 h** for every participant ([RimWorld wiki: Ritual](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ritual)). Roles matter mechanically: "moral guide present" is **+30%** quality on a funeral, "leader present" **+25%** on a speech.

Dwarf Fortress shows the opposite outcome for the same idea. DF has a full Performer occupation with musician, singer, poet, dancer and speaker skills, generated musical/poetic/dance forms, and visiting performance troupes — and the wiki's own verdict is that **"performers serve little practical purpose, as they mostly just duplicate the behavior of other taverngoers"**, with assigned performers not performing measurably more often than unassigned dwarves ([DF wiki: Performer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Performance), [DF wiki: Tavern](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Tavern)). Enormous generative depth, zero mechanical weight, because the *role assignment* changes nothing the sim would not have done anyway.

Frostpunk gates the whole game on two social meters, but their numbers are opaque. Hope 0% or Discontent ~100% triggers an ultimatum with **two days** to change the offending meter by **15%**, or the population mutinies and the player is banished ([gamepressure: what happens at critical values](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c); [Frostpunk wiki: Hope](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Hope), [Discontent](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Discontent)). Players reverse-engineering the system concluded most effects last 24 hours, child deaths cost more hope than adult deaths, ceremonial funerals halve the hope loss, and building effects scale with the population inside the aura — while stating plainly that **"the exact numbers are unknown"** because the developers never published them ([Steam: What are the specific Discontent and Hope values?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)). Frostpunk 2 replaced hope/discontent with per-faction **Fervour** feeding a city-wide **Tension** meter, so radicalisation is attributed to a named group rather than to an anonymous average ([gamepressure: Frostpunk 2 factions](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/factions/zc113e2)). Lead designer Jakub Stokalski framed the original system around "creeping normality" — incremental ethical compromises ([TechRaptor interview](https://techraptor.net/gaming/features/how-11-bit-studios-created-society-survival-simulator-frostpunk)).

Banished is the minimal version: happiness is per-citizen, raised by markets, wells, taverns, trading posts, cemeteries and chapels, lowered by mines and quarries nearby, births and marriages raise it, deaths/sickness/starvation/freezing lower it, and the only mechanical consequence is that **unhappy citizens idle more, and the unhappier they are the longer they idle** ([Banished wiki: Citizens](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens)). One meter, one consequence, fully legible.

Tropico splits happiness into per-category needs including Fun, and crucially scores buildings by **service quality**, not mere availability — an entertainment building that exists but is bad does not satisfy the need ([Steam: how do I increase my average happiness](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/760680362677397452/)).

### (e) Legibility in text/ASCII interfaces

Brogue is the best-documented case, and its author states the tradeoff directly: ASCII is "limiting in the sense that you get only one font glyph and two colors per square, which forces some tough design decisions when there is a lot of secondary information to convey… but freeing in the sense that they convey spatial location and identity of all of the dungeon's contents with crystal clarity, and then leave the rest to a well seeded imagination", which is why "there's an entire line of the UI that is permanently dedicated to flavor text" and why "Brogue tries very hard to convince you to see its dungeon as a place rather than a system of equations" ([Brian Walker, RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/how-do-roguelikes-generate-levels)).

The screen budget is fixed and small ([BrogueCE `Rogue.h`](https://github.com/tmewett/BrogueCE/blob/master/src/brogue/Rogue.h)): 100 columns × 34 rows, of which a **20-column sidebar**, a **79×29 map**, **3 message lines**, and two reserved bottom rows (one permanently the flavour line). The sidebar is the game's index of everything relevant: entities listed nearest-first in concentric rings, ≥2 rows each so ~16 fit, monsters then items then flagged terrain, with `sidebarLocationList[ROWS]` mapping each screen row back to a map position so the sidebar doubles as the Tab-cycle target list. Hovering the map highlights the matching sidebar rows, and if the focused entity would not fit the routine recurses to force it in.

Two implementation details worth stealing. First, legibility is enforced in code, not by taste: `separateColors()` rejects foreground/background pairs below `MIN_COLOR_DIFF 600` on a Rec.709-weighted squared-channel-difference metric, iterating up to 10 rounds of 20% averaging toward black or white until the glyph separates from its cell. Second, cosmetic randomness runs on a **separate RNG stream** (`RNG_COSMETIC` vs `RNG_SUBSTANTIVE`), so shimmering terrain never perturbs seed reproducibility — and the shimmer deliberately skips the cell under the cursor so the tile being inspected holds still.

Brogue also refuses to hide the model. Combat prints the odds before you swing: `"%s has a %i%% chance to hit you, typically hits for %i%% of your current health, and at worst, could defeat you in %i hit%s."`, with the rule itself in a source comment — `hit probability = accuracy * 0.987 ^ defense`. The discoveries screen prints a Bayesian `(N%)` per unidentified item kind computed as `frequency * 100 / totalFrequency` over still-unidentified kinds. Message handling is likewise concrete: 3 visible lines, a `--MORE--` gate, an archive of 340 lines / 1360 entries under `M`, and `MAX_MESSAGE_REPEATS 100` collapsing repeats into a counter instead of spamming.

Direction of travel on glyphs versus tiles: Brogue CE added tiles with an explicit "gameplay is identical" guarantee and then added a **hybrid mode that keeps text for creatures and items while using tiles for everything else** ([CE CHANGELOG](https://github.com/tmewett/BrogueCE/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). The things the player makes decisions about stayed glyphs.

RimWorld reaches the same conclusion from the other side: "RimWorld has graphics like a novel has a typeface" ([Sylvester, GDC 2017](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/Presentations/Sylvester_Tynan_RimWorld_Contrarian_Ridiculous.pdf)), and The Sims uses thought bubbles rather than sentences because "if we used actual language, the game would flatten and shrink" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-genius-ai-behind-the-sims)).

### Storyteller / director systems (cross-cutting)

RimWorld's storyteller is "an algorithm that generates events from a categorized database, based on a few statistics about what's happening in the game"; Sylvester's stated intent is that **"the game tries to hurt but not kill the colony"** — raiders steal or kidnap and leave instead of wiping the colony, and the storyteller eases off when population drops — and that incidents are designed to **overlap** in time (toxic fallout lasts days, fires burn slowly, sieges persist) so that pressure compounds ([Game Developer: How RimWorld fleshes out the Dwarf Fortress formula](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)).

The threat budget is an explicit formula: `Raid Points = (Wealth Points + Pawn Points) × Difficulty × Starting Factor × Adaptation Factor`, floor **35**, cap **10,000**. Wealth points interpolate linearly from 0 at 14k wealth to 2,400 at 400k (≈1 point per 160.83 wealth) and saturate at 4,200 by 1M. Each colonist is worth 15 points at ≤10k wealth rising to 140 at 400k. The **Starting Factor** is 0.7 for the first 10 days and reaches 1.0 at day 40. The **Adaptation Factor** ranges 0.4-1.47, rising with time since the last colonist death or downing and dropping when they occur; difficulty presets scale its *impact* from 100% down to 40% ([RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points), [AI Storytellers](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_Storytellers)). Note the shape: **the sim's own success is the difficulty input**, and recent suffering buys grace.

Population is also directed rather than random. `PopulationIntent` interpolates over two curves — days at the location (flat after **day 10**) and colonist count (flat after **20 colonists**, prisoners counting as half) — and multiplies each incident's base chance. A wanderer-join at 20+ population drops to **0.02%** per check for Cassandra/Phoebe and **0.08%** for Randy, whose `minIncChancePopulationIntentFactor` is 0.20 instead of the default 0.05 ([AI Storytellers, Technical info](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_Storytellers)). The three storytellers differ mainly in pacing shape: Cassandra runs a rising tension curve, Phoebe lengthens gaps between disasters, Randy drops the arc entirely.

The academic framing: Björk & Juul, *Zero-Player Games — What We Talk about When We Talk about Players* (Philosophy of Computer Games, Madrid 2012), decompose "player" into subcomponents — is the player human, does the player have agency, does the player play over time, does the player appear to have intentionality — and argue that zero-player games are best understood as edge cases that expose how vague the term is ([full text](https://jesperjuul.net/text/zeroplayergames/)).

## 2. Patterns that recur

1. **The director budgets, it does not roll dice.** RimWorld computes a points budget from wealth + population and spends it on threats; Cassandra/Phoebe/Randy differ in the *shape* of spending over time, not in the event table ([Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)). Aurora, by contrast, has no director at all and gets its pacing purely from interrupts.
2. **Success is the difficulty input, and suffering buys grace.** RimWorld's Adaptation Factor (0.4-1.47) rises with days since a colonist died or was downed and drops when one is; the Starting Factor holds threats at 0.7 for the first 10 days ([Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)). Sylvester's stated rule is "hurt but not kill" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)).
3. **Mood is a lagging indicator with an instantaneous target.** RimWorld separates mood target (instant) from mood bar (+12/−8 per hour); DF separates short-term stress (mood face) from long-term stress (years-long). Both refuse to let a single bad event break anyone ([Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood), [DF Stress](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Stress)).
4. **Variety beats quantity: monotony is modelled explicitly.** RimWorld tolerance rises at 2/3 of gain and blocks a type above 50%; the required number of distinct recreation types rises 2 → 6 with wealth ([Recreation](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Recreation)). Tropico scores service *quality*, not availability.
5. **Unmet needs are taxed in productivity, not only in mood.** DF focus is a ±50% skill multiplier driven purely by needs ([DF Need](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need)); Banished unhappiness buys idle time ([Banished](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens)); RimWorld parties give +20% global work speed for 24 h ([Ritual](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ritual)).
6. **Interrupts are a named, editable set.** DCSS enumerates both delay types and interrupt types and lets the player add regex rules ([DCSS options](https://crawl.akrasiac.org/docs/options_guide.txt)); Aurora's interrupt list ends auto-turns and cannot be disabled; CK2 let players route each message type. Every system that lasted made "what counts as an event" data, not code.
7. **Decelerate before the event, not at it.** KSP stops warp one minute early ([KSP forums](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/107407-warp-to-next-maneuver-node-improvements/)); RimWorld drops to 1x when a threat appears.
8. **The log becomes an artifact.** RimWorld engraves colony events onto sculptures ([Art](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Art)); CK2's Chronicle is written by explicit `on_action` hooks rather than scraped from the message feed ([CK2 wiki: Chronicle](https://ck2.paradoxwikis.com/Chronicle)); Qud renders generated history into gospels and relics.
9. **Influence is a scarce, unreliable budget — and reliability is sold separately.** Godville: a full godpower bar buys 2-4 acts, voice obedience ~30% in the arena, and the only 100% lever costs a 1000-brick temple. Pecorella's independent number for idle games is one meaningful decision on a 5-minute-to-hours cooldown. Nobody who succeeded gave the watcher a reliable button.
10. **Abstract feedback is a feature.** Sims thought bubbles instead of language, RimWorld's "graphics like a novel has a typeface", Zubek's weighted-random-over-top-N to avoid looking deterministic. The watcher completes the story only if the surface underdetermines it.
11. **Belief is a small card set with mechanical teeth.** RimWorld ideoligions take at most **4 memes**, have **19 mandatory issues**, and a *fluid* ideoligion starts with 1 meme and earns development points (1 for a good ritual outcome, 2 for a great one, 1 per conversion) to buy Reformations ([Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion)). DF encodes culture as entity ethics plus 0-100 personality facets, where a gap >60 vs <40 between two individuals seeds grudges ([DF Personality trait](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Personality_trait)).

## 3. Failure modes

- **A generative role with no mechanical hook is decorative.** DF's Performer occupation has skills, generated forms and visiting troupes, and the wiki still says performers "serve little practical purpose" because assigned performers do not perform more than unassigned ones ([DF Performer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Performance)). Depth of content does not create weight; only changing an outcome does.
- **Opaque social meters cannot be learned.** Six years on, Frostpunk players still write "the exact numbers are unknown" and infer hope deltas from funerals halving death penalties ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)). A meter the watcher cannot form a causal model of reads as arbitrary punishment.
- **Removing notification filtering is a visible regression.** CK3 shipped without CK2's per-message settings and the top community fixes are notification mods ([Paradox forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/notification-settings.1445086/), [Less Event Spam](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2750102888)).
- **Forced deceleration on trivial events becomes an irritant.** RimWorld's hard 1x lock during any hostile event produced years of "disable forced 1x speed" threads and a dedicated mod ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/1750150188485136328/)).
- **Fast-forwarding from a stale prediction overshoots.** KSP auto-warp misses distant nodes by hours ([MechJeb2 #402](https://github.com/MuMech/MechJeb2/issues/402)). If our accelerator picks a target tick from a forecast, the forecast must be re-checked every sub-step.
- **Anonymous averages hide the story.** Frostpunk 2 replaced the single hope/discontent pair with per-faction Fervour feeding city Tension precisely so unrest has an author ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/factions/zc113e2)).
- **One over-advertised activity eats the sim.** The Sims' Joy Booth was tuned so high that Sims used it until they collapsed — "a kind of SimHeroin" ([Forbus & Wright](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c95-gd/2001/Programming%20Objects%20in%20The%20Sims.pdf)). Directly relevant: our entertainers are exactly such an object. Zubek's companion failures — "bird in hand" (distance attenuation makes agents always take a worse nearby option) and **false advertisements creating unbreakable action loops** ([Needs-Based AI](https://zubek.net/robert/publications/Needs-based-AI-draft.pdf)).
- **More direct control degraded a beloved autonomous agent.** Molyneux agreed that Black & White 2's control sliders "ruined the creature as a person" ([archived GameSpot](https://www.bwgame.net/threads/some-comments-from-peter-molyneux-about-bw2.2157/)).
- **Punishing absence kills idle games.** Egg Inc's 2-hour offline cap churned out the genre's own leading designer, who calls it "a mistake" ([GDC Europe 2016 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf)).
- **A sandbox with no goal collapses into destruction.** WorldBox sessions default to the disaster tools because destruction is the only instant feedback ([Wikipedia: WorldBox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)).
- **Endless is a failure mode; sunk cost is not retention.** Clicker Heroes 2's postmortem — "a lot of the new game mechanics were risky decisions, which in the end, turned out to be the wrong choices", "we spent much more money on the project than we made from it", 53% Steam reviews, delisted 2024 ([Playsaurus](https://blog.playsaurus.com/index.php/2021/06/28/the-state-of-clicker-heroes-2/), [delistedgames](https://delistedgames.com/clicker-heroes-2/)). Against that, Lantz deliberately ended Universal Paperclips and calls the ending his proudest part.
- **Simulation cost caps tempo.** DF's 403,200 ticks/year at a default 100 FPS cap is 67 real minutes per year *before* a large fort slows the tick rate ([Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time), [Init.txt](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Init.txt)). Our few-minutes-per-year target is a hard budget on per-citizen work per tick.

## 4. Proposals for our design

Each item names the source mechanic, the mapping, and what it conflicts with.

1. **Chronicle entries come from explicit hooks, not from a filtered firehose.** Source: CK2 Chronicle written by `on_action` triggers ([CK2](https://ck2.paradoxwikis.com/Chronicle)); contrast the DF announcement stream. Mapping: define a closed `ChronicleEvent` enum (birth, death, first frost, ritual outcome, tradition adopted, mayor decree obeyed/ignored, defection) with a hand-written template per variant. Every other simulation event stays out of the chronicle entirely. Conflicts with: any wish for the chronicle to be "complete" — it will be deliberately lossy, and a separate debug log must exist for developers.
2. **Three-tier routing per event type, player-editable.** Source: CK2 message settings and DCSS `explore_stop` / `interrupt_<delay>` lists ([DCSS](https://crawl.akrasiac.org/docs/options_guide.txt)). Mapping: each `ChronicleEvent` variant carries a default routing of `Pause | Slow | Line | Silent`, overridable in a config file. This is the same table the tempo controller reads, so "what is worth reading" and "what is worth slowing for" are one dataset. Conflicts with: a fixed narrative arc — if the player silences everything, auto-acceleration never stops.
3. **Auto-tempo as an interrupt loop, with a pre-roll.** Source: Aurora auto-turns + KSP's one-minute pre-node stop. Mapping: the clock advances in the largest increment for which no scheduled event exists, but never past the next scheduled event minus a pre-roll of one game hour, so the watcher sees the approach. Recompute the horizon every sub-step because the sim can create events mid-increment. Conflicts with: fixed "one year per N minutes" pacing — actual tempo will vary widely and the year-length promise becomes an average, not a guarantee.
4. **Two mood numbers, not one.** Source: RimWorld mood target vs mood bar (+12/−8 per hour) and DF short vs long-term stress ([Mood](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mood), [Stress](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Stress)). Mapping: per citizen keep `mood_target` (instant sum of thoughts) and `mood` (chases it, faster up than down); keep a slow `hardship` accumulator with named statuses at fixed thresholds. At our tempo the rise/fall rates must be expressed per game *day*, not per hour, or every citizen will be at target every frame. Conflicts with: the fast clock — asymmetric lag is only visible if the lag is long relative to the compressed hour.
5. **Entertainers are load-bearing via a ritual-quality formula, not via a job slot.** Source: RimWorld ritual quality as a sum of named contributions with an outcome distribution and a work-speed payout ([Ritual](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ritual)); anti-source: DF's decorative Performer. Mapping: an entertainer's performance produces a Quality percentage from enumerated terms (audience size, venue warmth, instrument availability, entertainer skill, tradition match), Quality maps to an outcome tier, and the tier pays out in mood **and** a colony-wide work-speed buff for a fixed number of days **and** culture points. Conflicts with: the cold-survival fiction — a labour-hour spent entertaining is a labour-hour not spent on coal, so the payout must be tuned to be marginal in a crisis and dominant in a plateau, which is exactly where the numbers will be hardest to balance.
6. **Culture cards are bought with ritual outcomes.** Source: RimWorld fluid ideoligion development points — 1 point per good ritual outcome, 2 per great, 1 per conversion, spent on Reformations, with a cap of 4 memes ([Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion)). Mapping: entertainers generate the currency that buys tradition cards; cap the active card set low (4-6) so the culture is readable as a sentence. Conflicts with: "invisible on the map" — if cards are invisible AND their acquisition is slow, the player may never notice them; each adoption must produce a chronicle line.
7. **Rising expectations tied to prosperity.** Source: RimWorld's wealth-indexed tolerance decay (18%/day at <15k wealth → 7%/day above 308k) and the recommended 2 → 6 recreation types ([Recreation](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Recreation)). Mapping: the number of distinct entertainment kinds needed to hold mood scales with colony prosperity, and repeating the same kind builds tolerance at 2/3 of the gain with a boredom lock-out band (enter at 50%, exit at 30%). Conflicts with: small colonies — with few citizens there may not be enough entertainers to field the required variety, so the requirement should key off prosperity per capita.
8. **Needs pay in output, not only in mood.** Source: DF focus as a ±50% skill multiplier with weighted needs (1/2/5/10) and satisfy-intervals of 3/6/12/24 months ([DF Need](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Need)). Mapping: a citizen with unmet needs works slower before they become unhappy, so the chronicle can report a productivity dip as foreshadowing rather than reporting the breakdown after the fact. Conflicts with: legibility — two coupled meters per citizen is already at the edge of what an ASCII sidebar can show.
9. **A three-scale event ribbon, not just a scrolling log.** Source: RimWorld's History graph with coloured event circles filterable to 300/100/30 days ([Menus](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Menus)). Mapping: under the map, one line of glyphs per season with colour by valence (neutral / negative / crisis), expandable to per-day and per-hour. Gives "what kind of year was this" at a glance, which prose cannot do at our tempo. Conflicts with: pure-text purity — it needs stable colour semantics and therefore a colour-capable terminal.
10. **The chronicle feeds culture back into the world.** Source: RimWorld sculptures depicting real colony events from an enumerated list ([Art](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Art)); Qud rendering generated history into in-world texts ([Qud paper](https://www.freeholdgames.com/papers/Generation_of_mythic_biographies_in_Cavesofqud.pdf)). Mapping: entertainers turn past chronicle entries into songs/stories that are themselves objects with mood value, so a bad winter later becomes a positive cultural asset. Conflicts with: template fatigue — a small grammar reused across a long game will be recognised, so grammar size must scale with expected session length.
11. **Legitimacy as an ultimatum clock, with a named cause.** Source: Frostpunk's "change the meter by 15% within 2 days or be exiled" ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c)); corrective from Frostpunk 2's per-faction Fervour ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/factions/zc113e2)). Mapping: when legitimacy crosses a floor, emit a chronicle entry naming the group and the grievance and a deadline in game-days; the mayor's directives are ignored more often until it is resolved. Conflicts with: the zero-player premise — a hard deadline demands action, so the resolution must be reachable by *nudges* rather than by direct commands, or the game stops being a zero-player game at exactly its most interesting moment.
12. **The mayor is a budget, not a command line, and obedience is probabilistic.** Source: Godville's godpower economy (2-4 acts per full bar, ~30% obedience on voice, Miracle as the expensive 100% exception, enforced no-influence windows); anti-source: Black & White 2's sliders. Mapping: legitimacy *is* the budget — it refills slowly, each nudge spends it, and legitimacy sets the **probability** a directive is followed rather than its magnitude. Reserve exactly one deterministic action, made expensive. Add at least one recurring window (a crisis, a festival) where the mayor cannot act at all. Conflicts with: player frustration — an unreliable lever must be paired with a chronicle line that *names* why it was ignored, or it reads as a bug.
13. **Cap entertainment's advertisement.** Source: the Joy Booth collapse and Zubek's false-advertisement loops. Mapping: whatever score entertainment advertises to a citizen's decision-maker must be bounded and must fall as the need fills, and the arbiter should be weighted-random over the top N rather than winner-takes-all so citizens do not all converge on the same venue. Conflicts with: the entertainer's value proposition — the same cap that prevents a death spiral also limits how load-bearing entertainers can be.
14. **Chronicle as a rolling feed plus a sparse permanent record.** Source: Godville's hard 10-entry diary versus CK2's per-reign Chronicle. Mapping: the on-screen log is a short rolling window (tens of lines) the watcher may miss, while a separate, much sparser permanent chronicle keeps only hook-emitted milestones and is browsable by year. Conflicts with: the "main way the player reads what is happening" goal — at our tempo a rolling feed will scroll faster than anyone reads, so the permanent record, not the feed, carries comprehension.
15. **Plan an ending.** Source: Lantz's deliberate finish versus the endless-idle failures; Sylvester's "loss is an essential part of a story, not its conclusion". Mapping: define a terminal state the colony can reach (the winter that ends, the city that outlives its founders) and treat colony death as a chronicle chapter with a legible epitaph rather than a game-over screen. Conflicts with: the sandbox impulse to let it run forever.
16. **Budget the terminal explicitly and make the sidebar the index.** Source: Brogue's fixed 100×34 split — 20-col sidebar, 79×29 map, 3 message lines, one permanent flavour line — with the sidebar listing entities nearest-first and mapping each row back to a map position ([Rogue.h](https://github.com/tmewett/BrogueCE/blob/master/src/brogue/Rogue.h)). Mapping: reserve a fixed sidebar for the citizens/facts currently mattering most, and let the chronicle occupy a bounded number of lines with an archive key, `--MORE--`-style gating, and repeat-collapsing (Brogue caps repeats at 100 and shows a counter). Conflicts with: showing per-citizen mood for a large colony — the sidebar caps at roughly 16 entries, so ranking is mandatory.
17. **Two RNG streams, and enforce contrast in code.** Source: Brogue's `RNG_COSMETIC` vs `RNG_SUBSTANTIVE`, and `separateColors()` refusing pairs below a Rec.709-weighted difference threshold, iterating up to 10 rounds until the glyph separates. Mapping: any cosmetic flicker in our ASCII output must not touch the simulation seed, and colour choice should be validated programmatically rather than eyeballed. Conflicts with: nothing — this is cheap and prevents an entire class of "the run isn't reproducible" bugs.
18. **Publish the numbers.** Anti-source: Frostpunk's undocumented hope values ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)); positive source: RimWorld's fully documented, community-reverse-engineered formulas. Mapping: every mood, legitimacy and culture delta should be inspectable in-game as a named list of contributions, the way RimWorld's thought list and ritual-quality breakdown are. For a game whose whole content is watching, the causal model *is* the gameplay. Conflicts with: nothing except screen space, which in an ASCII layout is the binding constraint.

## 5. What I could not source

- Frostpunk's actual hope/discontent numbers. The developers never published them; the community's own reverse-engineering thread states plainly that "the exact numbers are unknown", so every Frostpunk delta above is a threshold or a rule, never a coefficient. The Frostpunk wiki is behind a Cloudflare challenge for automated fetching, so its pages are cited but were read only through search summaries.
- Aurora 4X's exact list of interrupt event types and increment sizes. The canonical wiki (`aurorawiki.pentarch.org`) returned HTTP 522 throughout, and the Fandom mirror is challenge-gated; the auto-turns and sub-pulse behaviour is cited from search summaries of that mirror, not from a direct read.
- Godville's server tick rate and any audience/revenue figures from Platov or Kosinov. Duel timing (~40 s per move) came from a search summary; the GodWiki page itself is Cloudflare-gated.
- Progress Quest: no statements by Eric Fredricksen exist online that I could find — grumdrig.com is empty and there are no interviews.
- The Sims: the numeric mapping from the Free Will slider (Off/Low/High) to autonomy thresholds. `sims.fandom.com` returns 403; Richard Evans' and Jamie Doornbos' GDC talks are paywalled.
- WorldBox: tick rate, population caps, world dimensions, and any developer design interview — none found.
- Banished and Tropico: no numeric happiness formulas are published for either; both entries above are qualitative and should not be treated as balanced numbers.
- Brogue: the three long-form Brian Walker interviews are audio/video only with no transcripts, so anything he said there is unverified. The widely repeated "Brogue has no hidden information" phrasing is community shorthand, not a Walker quote — what is actually sourced is narrower: disclosed hit probabilities, disclosed seeds, and an item pool fixed at game start.
- Cookie Clicker's Orteil quote on the "moment of sudden clarity" reached me through a search summary; polygon.com could not be fetched directly.
- The web-search budget for this session was exhausted before I could reach Caves of Qud's history generator in the depth it deserves; the Qud claims above rest on the Freehold Games paper and the GDC talk listing, not on a full read of either.
