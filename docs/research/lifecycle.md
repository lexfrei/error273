# Lifecycle, Time Scale and Job Assignment in Generational Colony Sims

Research for a zero-player ASCII colony sim (Rust/Bevy, nested clocks, per-citizen needs, mayor-mediated influence). Every mechanic claim carries a source. Sentences marked "(from memory, unverified)" have no source found.

## 1. Per-game findings

### Banished (Shining Rock Software, 2014)

- **(a) Time.** Community measurement puts one in-game year at roughly 25 real minutes at 1x (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/490125737479022592/); another thread reports ~10 minutes per year at 10x, so the community numbers do not reconcile cleanly and should be treated as an order-of-magnitude, not a spec. The important fact is structural: **citizen aging is deliberately decoupled from the calendar.** The wiki states "Citizens age 5 times per 1 in-game year, not once. This averages out to age roughly once per every 2.4 seasons" (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens); a widely-cited Steam thread instead reports "your citizens age a year per season" i.e. 4x (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/133261907131050467/). Sources disagree on 4x vs 5x; all agree the multiplier exists and that players find it "very counter-intuitive". A mod named "one year is one year" exists purely to remove it (https://banishedinfo.com/mods/view/27-one-year-is-one-year-Update-26-17091434).
- **(b) Aging.** Children become laborers at 10; with a staffed school they become students at 10 instead and graduate at 16-18, up to 23 if the school is far from home (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/540734255191466322/, https://banishedinfo.com/wiki/Education). Women bear children roughly 16-40 and **require their own house, not a parent's** — housing is the birth throttle (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/3044982312814656263/). Only women can die in childbirth; otherwise the sexes are mechanically identical and "Male and female citizens are equally likely to take any Profession and handle it with equal facility" (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens).
- **(c) Stats.** Deliberately thin: Name, Gender, Age, Job, Clothing, Education, Health, Happiness — no aptitude, no per-skill growth (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens). Education is a **binary** flag, worth roughly +30% productivity plus reduced fire and childbirth deaths (https://banishedinfo.com/wiki/Education). Happiness feeds productivity: "An unhappy population will idle frequently, decreasing overall production" (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens).
- **(d) Job assignment.** The player sets **headcounts per profession, never individuals**; unassigned citizens fall into a Laborer pool that absorbs all undirected work (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens). Selection is proximity-based and the developer describes the flaw himself: "they pick the closest accessible location and use it. While using closest linear distance works, the closest location isn't always the best. Sometimes walking distance far exceeds linear distance" (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-11-21-more-bugs-pathfinding-problems/). Patch 1.0.3 split the rule by class: "laborers don't have any constraints on when they'll go to do work or how far away it is", while "all other professions will still stick closer to their home and job unless a job is fairly old and no laborer has done it" (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2014-05-31-laborer-update/).
- **(e) Debuffs.** Uneducated workers produce ~30% less and are likelier to die in work accidents and childbirth (https://banishedinfo.com/wiki/Education); unhappiness converts to idling rather than a flat output multiplier (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens).
- **Designer note.** For his next game Shining Rock replaced the closest-job rule with utility scoring: "Each object gives a score to the villager as to how useful it is to perform it's actions, which will be combined with the desires of the villager", and "Considering all the possible actions available to them, the villager will pick one of the top ones" — top-N sampling, not argmax (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2021-02-16-designing-the-ai/).

### Dwarf Fortress (Bay 12)

- **(a) Time.** Fortress mode runs 1,200 ticks/day, 33,600/month, 100,800/season, 403,200/year; 28 days per month, 12 months, 4 seasons. At 100 FPS a day is 12 seconds and a year is 1h07m; at 30 FPS a year is 3h44m (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Time). **Real-time year length is a function of simulation load, not a design constant** — an accepted convention. One tick is 72 in-world seconds in fortress mode versus 0.5 seconds in adventure mode, a 144:1 ratio the game simply does not reconcile (same source).
- **(b) Aging.** Dwarves are infants to age 1, children to 12, then adults (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2010:Children). `[MAXAGE]` for dwarves is 150-170: death by old age is rolled at the start of each year in that window (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Age). Humans are 60-120; elves and goblins are immortal. Lifespan (150y) vastly exceeds a typical fortress run (a few in-game decades), so **old age is effectively decorative** for dwarves and only matters for other races.
- **(c) Stats — the strongest model for our hidden-stat design.** 6 physical + 13 mental attributes, each rolled on a species range, e.g. dwarven Strength `[450:950:1150:1250:1350:1550:2250]` and Focus `[700:1200:1400:1500:1600:1800:2500]` (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Attribute). **The numbers are never shown.** The UI prints descriptive text bucketed at ±250/500/750/1000 from the *species* median, so "strong" means 1500-1749 for a dwarf but something else for a modded creature (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Attribute). Reading real values requires DFHack `gui/gm-editor` (https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/599643705297649536/). Attributes train by use: a counter increments every 10 ticks the relevant skill is in use and needs 500 increments per point, "approximately 5000 TUs or just over 4 in-game days of continuous labor" per point; cap is `starting_value + max{starting_value, median}` (same source). Skills are separate: 500 XP to Novice, +100 per subsequent rank, ~30 XP per use (10 for mining/engraving/smoothing); unused skills **rust** — "Very Rusty" at 75% rust layers, and gaining XP after rust destroys 50%+1 of the gained points (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Skill).
- **(d) Job assignment.** The classic mismatch generator, and the mechanism is documented: "A dwarf currently doing a job will when choosing the next job of that type always pick an accessible, designated tile with the minimum distance (in the maximum norm) from the current position, **regardless of how long the path to actually get there is**" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Labor). Chebyshev distance, not path cost — so a dwarf walks around a wall to reach the "nearest" tile. Priority overrides convenience entirely: "When a dwarf has several jobs of different priority to do, the ones with higher priority are executed first, regardless of how convenient they are for the dwarf" (same source).
- **(e) Debuffs — the needs→focus→skill chain is directly reusable.** Focus is "the percentage of the combined totals of met over unmet need values", 100% neutral, banded Very focused 140%+ / Focused 101-119% / Unfocused 81-99% / Distracted 61-80% / Badly distracted ≤60%. "A highly-focused dwarf can receive an effective-skill bonus of up to 50%; a badly distracted dwarf can receive an effective skill penalty of up to 50%" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Need). Crucially, **focus is independent of mood**: "a dwarf can be happy even with largely-unmet needs, or unhappy yet focused" (same source). Satisfying a need refreshes it to a flat maximum of 400 regardless of prior value.

### RimWorld (Ludeon)

- **(a) Time.** A day is 60,000 ticks = 16.67 real minutes at 1x (60 ticks/real second, 2,500 ticks per in-game hour); a year is 4 quadrums × 15 days = 60 days ≈ 16.7 real hours (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Time). So RimWorld's year is ~40x longer in real time than Banished's, and it compensates by never making old age matter.
- **(b) Aging.** Nominal life expectancy ~80 but **there is no death-by-old-age event**; elderly pawns accumulate health conditions and lose immunity-gain speed until some illness kills them (https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/4356745862493486100/, https://rimworldhub.com/post/rimworld_age_secrets_biological_vs_chronological_demystified). Biotech children: babies become children at 3, growth moments at 7, 10 and 13 unlock new work types and let the player pick traits and passions; 13-18 are teenagers counted as adults for most purposes; children are "smaller, slower, and less capable of work" than adults (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Children). Vat-grown children get "no passions and 3 random traits" — the game explicitly penalises skipping the upbringing (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Growth_vat).
- **(c) Stats.** 12 skills, 0-20, fully visible. Passion multiplies XP: none 35%, Interested 100% (+8 mood while working it), Burning 150% (+14 mood) (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Skills). Global Learning Factor offsets stack additively on 100% then multiply (Fast Learner +75%, Slow Learner −75%). Soft cap of 4,000 net XP/day/skill, overflow at 20%. **Decay above level 10**: XP drains continuously, a level is lost only after hitting 0 and losing a further 1,000, so high skill needs continuous practice (same source). Wiki's own strategy note: 150,000 XP buys one level-16 master or three level-9 workers, and three level-9s are usually better.
- **(d) Job assignment.** Player sets a 1-4 priority grid per pawn per work type; within a priority, order is left-to-right in the tab. The friction is designed in and the wiki says so plainly: "all available work of one priority will be done before the next priority. If Hauling is set to 1, then a colonist will haul every single object, even those halfway across the map, before doing the rest of their tasks. **They have no regard for efficiency**" (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Work).
- **(e) Debuffs.** Low skill ≠ incapable. "At level 0, a pawn is still capable of performing the task, they will just be extremely slow, ineffective, inefficient, and have an increased chance of failure" — construction failure, surgery failure, wild-animal revenge, low harvest yield, poor craft quality (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Work, https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Skills). "Incapable of" is the hard gate, sourced from traits, backstories, titles, slave status or ideoligion, and stacks across sources until a pawn can be "incapable of practically everything" (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Work). The UI plays "a crunching sound... when you assign a pawn to a work type which they won't do well due to low skill" — a deliberate feedback channel for mismatch.
- **Designer framing.** Sylvester's GDC talk frames RimWorld as "designed not as a game, but as a story generator", built by "strategically leaving out features" so that "players engage with features and story elements that aren't actually there" (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-i-rimworld-i-found-success-through-ridiculous-contrarian-design).

### Oxygen Not Included (Klei) — bonus, not on the brief but the cleanest aptitude/priority model

- **(c) Stats.** 11 attributes, each trainable to +20, with published per-level effects: Construction +25% build speed/level, Excavation +25% dig speed, Science +40% research, Athletics +10% run speed, Cuisine +5% cooking (https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Duplicant). Each duplicant gets 1-3 **interests**, and the starting bonus is inversely scaled: one interest +7, two +3 each, three +1 each — specialists start sharper than generalists (same source). Traits are balanced by a rarity-point budget summing toward 0.
- **(d) Assignment.** A 1-9 priority grid crossed with per-building priority; ties break on a hidden sub-priority and then on distance, and the distance term is **player-toggleable** (https://oxygennotincluded.fandom.com/wiki/Priority). Two friction rules matter: "A busy Duplicant will continue to work on their current errand until it's complete, even if a higher priority errand becomes available", and "**no matter what priorities you have set, Duplicants will always take care of their personal needs**" (same source).
- **(e) Debuffs.** Skills cost morale: "Learning a Skill will increase the Duplicant's total morale need by the Skill's numeric tier", reduced by 1 if the duplicant is interested in that work type (https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Skills). Promoting someone into work they don't like is therefore a standing morale tax — an elegant inversion of the usual "skill is free".

### Timberborn (Mechanistry)

- **(a) Time.** A day is **7m40.8s real at 1x** (2m33.6s at 2x, 1m05.8s at 3x), and speed multipliers **degrade with population** — at 200+ beavers "fastest" is only 3.4x (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Time). A day is 24 beaver-hours = **768 ticks** (32 ticks/hour), 16h day / 8h night, with a player-adjustable 0-24h working day. Seasons are difficulty-scaled: Normal wet season 13-17 days, drought 5-9 days; Hard drought 15-30 days (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mode). Severity ramps in: Normal droughts start at 38% of full length and reach 100% over 5 cycles. Construction has **no fixed duration** — it is material-delivery-gated in 10% steps, then builder-hours (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Construction).
- **(b) Aging.** Kit → adult in **6 days**; base lifespan **50 days ±10%**; beavers age "by one every dawn" and do die of old age (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Kits, https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Life_Expectancy). Folktails breed only when two adults share a Lodge and an unmet Shelter need **blocks reproduction entirely**; Iron Teeth instead use a Breeding Pod producing 1 kit per 5 days for 5 water + 5 berries, and an Advanced Pod that outputs adults directly (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Breeding_Pod). **The demographic pyramid is an output, not an input**: high well-being shortens childhood and extends adult life, so kits fall from 12.00% to 3.12% of population as well-being rises (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Kits).
- **(c) Stats.** Beavers are named individuals carrying their own Movement Speed, Life Expectancy, Well-Being, age and job (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Beavers). Well-Being is a single per-beaver score accumulated from satisfied needs, and it scales **everything**: at score 70-71 a beaver gets **+260% work speed, +75% growth, +65% movement, +120% life expectancy**, pushing max lifespan to ~110 days versus 50 base (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/index.php?title=Category:Well-Being&action=raw).
- **(d) Assignment.** Fully automatic and priority-driven: "Workers are assigned from the ranks of the unemployed in order of workplace priority... Workplaces with a very high workplace priority are filled first" — low-priority workplaces simply starve when workers run out (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Workplaces). Slots are 1 (Gristmill, Inventor) to 10 (Mine, Hauling Post) per building (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Workers). **There is no per-slot priority and no way to pick an individual beaver** — both are top-voted feature requests (https://timberborn.featureupvote.com/suggestions/202922/better-way-to-add-and-manage-workers-individual-beavers-select-home-and-job).
- **(e) Debuffs.** Needs decay per day at fixed rates (Hunger −80/day, Thirst −70, Sleep −60, Shelter −30, Injury −10) giving **5 days from full hunger to death**, 5.71 from full thirst (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Needs). Penalties are **binary, not graduated**: Starving flips −50% work speed and −40% growth; Thirsty flips −25% movement; Injured causes **immediate unemployment** for 10 days.

### Frostpunk (11 bit studios) — closest tonal reference

- **Irreversible law commitment.** A signed law "cannot be unsigned", and an **18-hour in-game cooldown** between signings prevents law-spam (https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Book_of_Laws).
- **Child labour is a two-step ladder, not a toggle**: Safe Jobs (Cookhouses/Hothouses, small Hope loss, children can be injured) → 18h cooldown → All Jobs (most workplaces, Hope falls and Discontent rises, children can be injured **or die**), 36h cooldown, with a scripted event arc attached (https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Child_Labour_-_Safe_Jobs_(Adaptation_Law), https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Child_Labour_(Arc)).
- **Two opposed 0-100 meters**: Hope (lose at 0) and Discontent (lose at 100). Sustained failure exiles you rather than ending the run instantly, with a grace window of roughly "shift it 15% within 2 days" (https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Hope, https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Discontent).

### Against the Storm (Eremite) — best species/job-matching model found

- **Two decoupled matching bonuses, and this is the design worth stealing.** **Proficiency** gives a 10% chance of double output in tagged buildings (Beavers→woodworking, Humans→farming, Harpies→alchemy). **Comfort** gives **+5 Resolve** while working in a *different* tagged set (Humans→brewing, Harpies→cloth). Output efficiency and mood are deliberately **not** the same axis (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Humans, https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Beavers).
- Each of five species has its own needs list feeding **Resolve**; above a threshold a villager generates Reputation, below a floor they leave (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Resolve).
- **Queen's Impatience** is a real-time doom clock at 0.255 points/minute, lose at 14; a Human Firekeeper cuts it 25%, and each Reputation point removes 1. The failure meter has a **compensating upside** — Impatience also lowers forest hostility (https://hoodedhorse.com/wiki/Against_the_Storm/Queen's_Impatience).

### Norland, Farthest Frontier, Going Medieval, The Sims (lifecycle dials)

- **Norland**: characters age **1 year per 2 real days**, max age in the **50s**; lords gain **double lesson XP until age 21**, a direct incentive to school heirs. An heir whose loyalty hits 0 while unfriendly with the king can **rebel**, splitting the army evenly (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Norland/Population, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1857090/discussions/1/4524512657551140109/). Players widely complain aging is too fast and ask for a slider (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1857090/discussions/1/573793962962268326/).
- **Farthest Frontier**: a **two-rate aging curve** — children age fast, then adults age 1 year per game year; the elderly can die of old age but **stay employable** (https://www.farthestfrontier.com/guide/villagers/population/).
- **Going Medieval**: mood is a stack of **timed memory modifiers**; low mood makes settlers refuse orders and **mood 0 means the settler leaves**, not dies (https://goingmedieval.fandom.com/wiki/Mood).
- **The Sims 4** treats lifespan as a pure tuning dial: Normal = 2 baby / 7 toddler / 13 child / 21 teen / 24 young adult / 33 adult / 14 elder = **117 days**; Short = 47 days; Long ≈ 4x Normal — same stage table, three global multipliers (https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Lifespan).

### Lords & Villeins (Honestly Games) — the closest existing model for indirect, family-mediated control

- The player **never commands individuals**: "you don't manage the daily life of every villager; instead, you deal with whole families and their descendants", assigning plots and taxation while families "do so almost completely autonomously, all the while managing their stock, setting aside produce to pay taxes, and trading with other families" (https://store.steampowered.com/app/1287530/Lords_And_Villeins/).
- **Profession is a property of the family and is inherited, not chosen**: "The profession of a family determined what job they can do. This is determined by the game and cannot be removed", and a spouse marrying in "will learn the family's profession and assist with the workload" (https://wiki.lordsandvilleins.com/subdom/wiki/index.php?title=Professions).
- Social class gates professions and raises standard-of-living demands: 17 peasant professions, 5 noble, 7 clergy, 3 NPC-only (same source).

### Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic (3Division)

- **(a) Time.** 1 calendar day = **60 real seconds at 1x**, so a month ≈30 real minutes and a year ≈**6.08 real hours** (https://steamcommunity.com/app/784150/discussions/0/4513255594049767022/). A citizen's day is a fixed budget: **8h work or education, 16h free time, 4h travel to work**; a shift starts when the worker *arrives*, so a longer commute extends the shift (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). Construction is priced in **workdays** — Kindergarten 394-511, School 511-975, Technical university **5188** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Construction).
- **(b) Aging.** Citizens age **12 years per in-game year** (+1 age per in-game month), so a full life fits in ~6-7 in-game years (https://steamcommunity.com/app/784150/discussions/0/3103515084625734656/). Brackets: babies 0-6, children 7-21, then workers — and **there is no retirement bracket, citizens work until they die** (same source). Lifespan is driven by Health: ~90%+ health gives ~80 years, polluted areas ~70-74 (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). A couple needs its own flat; 21+ citizens still with parents don't reproduce and **flee the republic if no housing exists anywhere** (same source). Education is a 0.0-3.0 scale: ≥1.0 basic, ≥2.0 university; in complex mode a missing kindergarten slot means **a parent stays home and is lost from the workforce** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Education_simulation).
- **(c) Stats.** Visible: age, lifespan, education, plus bars for satiety, health, culture, sport, alcohol, religion. **Loyalty is hidden until the Secret Police install spy equipment in the home**, and unknown-loyalty citizens are allowed into sensitive jobs by default; **criminality is a hidden randomly-generated preference** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). There is **no skill-by-practice growth at all** — education is the only competence axis. Community-reverse-engineered productivity: `(1 − 0.8×(1−happiness)) × (1 − 0.225×(1−food)) × (1 − 0.27×(1−health)) × (1 − 0.52×(1−loyalty)) × 1.5`, floored at **30%** and capped at **150%** (https://steamcommunity.com/app/784150/discussions/0/4338725580147992787/).
- **(d) Assignment.** **Workers have no fixed job.** Every morning each worker searches for any building with a free slot within a **~450 m walking radius** (250 m over mud, 411 m over gravel), else heads for a transit stop; because slots run 24/7 on 8h shifts you need **~3x more workers than job slots** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). Education **hard-gates** slots — buildings declare separate `workers` and `workers_high_education` counts (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/School).
- **(e) Debuffs.** Uneducated workers **cannot work at all** in the gated jobs — it is a gate, not a multiplier (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). Note: the widely circulated "50% penalty for uneducated / 66% for overqualified" figure is **a player's forum proposal, not a mechanic** (https://steamcommunity.com/app/784150/discussions/0/1649917420733301968/). Unmet demand costs happiness on each roll with an **amplified penalty once the need drops below 5%**; escapes trigger below ~80% happiness but max satiety plus max loyalty can cancel the decision (same source).

### Ostriv (Yevheniy Yankovskyi) — the strongest designer statement against optimal assignment

- **(a) Time.** Base timescale is **1440x**: 1 in-game day ≈ 1 real minute, a month = **30 real minutes**, a year = **6 real hours** at 1x; at the 12x tier a year is 30 minutes (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Seasons). Seasons are **hard calendar gates, not modifiers**: fields sow the instant March starts, cows enter cowsheds in early November, and **anything unharvested on 30 November is destroyed**. Construction has no timer — buildings assemble **part by part**, and the saltworks has **556 hand-modelled parts** ordered by construction sequence (https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/).
- **(b) Aging.** Citizens were **immortal until Alpha 2 (May 2019)**, which added death by old age and burial plots (https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-2-released/). Children 10-15 attend primary school (max 10 per school), **15-18 work as laborers at reduced productivity**, 18+ are full workers (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Primary_school). Aging is **1:1 with the calendar**, so generational turnover needs decades of play. Inheritance is explicit: children inherit from deceased parents, families **share money with relatives living separately**, and when both parents die **the oldest resident child becomes head of family** (https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-4-released/, https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-5-released/). Elderly living alone try to move in with their children.
- **(c) Stats.** The sim has stored **per-citizen experience for every type of work since May 2017** (https://ostrivgame.com/status-report-and-closed-testing-announcement/). Alpha 5 added a per-citizen health metric plus illnesses that switch on at **250 population**, a **personal log of significant life events**, a family tree, and clickable "thoughts" (https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-5-released/). Household wealth is a first-class simulated quantity. **Sex is a hard job constraint**: forestry, builders, smithy and trading post are male-only; thatchery, orchards and farms take women (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Tips_%26_Strategies).
- **(d) Assignment — read this one carefully, it is our design.** Citizens pick their own jobs via a `checkForBetterJob` function weighing **distance, prior experience with that job type, and wage**, with a strict 3500-unit distance limit added after cross-map-commuting complaints. The developer's stated reason for refusing direct assignment: *"you can't just assign someone to work in specific place and live with specific spouse because, you know, that's mathematically effective"* (https://steamcommunity.com/app/773790/discussions/0/2268069450224718072/?ctp=2). **Player influence is entirely indirect and economic**: a town-wide basic wage, a managers' wage (+50%), a laborers' wage, and a **per-building wage percentage** to make one workplace more attractive than another (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Town_hall). Laborers (construction, hauling, gathering) are drawn from the unemployed pool, and guidance is to keep **20-25% of the workforce unemployed** as reserve (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Laborers).
- **(e) Debuffs.** 15-18-year-olds work at reduced productivity; women use carts at reduced efficiency (https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-4-released/). The laborer wage is separate and lower by default, so **setting it too low starves construction** because citizens prefer any vacant regular slot (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Economy). Emigration is the main penalty channel, triggered per family by lack of water, food, firewood, money, or inability to buy shoes/clothes/warm clothes (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Villager).

## 2. Patterns that recur across games

1. **Aging is decoupled from the calendar in almost every game, and players tolerate it as long as the lie is consistent.** Banished ages citizens 4-5x per calendar year (https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Citizens); Timberborn's entire lifespan is 50 days (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Life_Expectancy); Norland runs 1 year per 2 real days with a max age in the 50s (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1857090/discussions/1/4524512657551140109/); DF's own tick means 72 in-world seconds in fortress mode and 0.5 seconds in adventure mode, a 144:1 ratio it never reconciles (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Time). The pattern: **pick the lifespan that fits your intended session length first, then bend the calendar to it** — do not derive lifespan from a "realistic" year.
2. **Nobody computes an optimal worker-to-job assignment. Every game uses a cheap, deliberately approximate proximity rule, and that approximation is the mismatch generator.** DF picks minimum Chebyshev distance "regardless of how long the path to actually get there is" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Labor); Banished picked minimum straight-line distance and the developer publicly identified it as a flaw (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-11-21-more-bugs-pathfinding-problems/); ONI breaks ties on distance and makes the distance term a **toggle** (https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Priority); Timberborn fills workplaces by priority order and lets low-priority buildings simply starve (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Workplaces).
3. **Priority beats convenience, unconditionally, and this is stated as a feature.** RimWorld: "They have no regard for efficiency" (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Work). DF: higher priority first "regardless of how convenient they are for the dwarf in question" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Labor). This is the most reliable source of legible, explicable mismatch in the genre.
4. **Personal needs are the one hard interrupt that overrides all assignment.** ONI: "no matter what priorities you have set, Duplicants will always take care of their personal needs" (https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Priority). Timberborn: injury causes immediate unemployment (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Needs). Every game in the set has this and none make it configurable.
5. **Unmet needs are converted into a work-quality multiplier, not merely a mood number — and there are two distinct designs.** DF multiplies **effective skill** by ±50% via focus and keeps it explicitly independent of happiness (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Need). Timberborn multiplies raw **output** — +260% work speed at high well-being (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/index.php?title=Category:Well-Being&action=raw). DF's version is stronger for a mismatch-centred design because a tired worker and a badly-fitted worker then fail in the same currency and produce the same kind of story.
6. **Hidden numeric stats ship as bucketed adjectives, never numbers.** DF is the only shipped model in the set: descriptions shift at ±250/500/750/1000 from the **species** median, so the same word means different numbers for different creatures, and reading the real value requires DFHack (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Attribute).
7. **Skill decays, so specialists cost upkeep and reassignment is a real tax.** RimWorld drains XP continuously above level 10 (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Skills); DF rusts unused skills and destroys "50% of the gained experience points (rounded down, plus 1)" when re-training a rusty skill (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Skill). This is what makes "nobody quits on their own" load-bearing rather than arbitrary.
8. **The best-output job and the happiest job should not be the same building.** Against the Storm splits them explicitly: Proficiency (10% double output) and Comfort (+5 Resolve) point at **different** tagged building sets (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Humans).
9. **Demographics work best as an output of colony health, not an input.** In Timberborn high well-being shortens childhood and extends adult life, so kits fall from 12.00% to 3.12% of population without the player ever setting a birth rate (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Kits).
10. **Construction is gated on material delivery, not on a timer.** Timberborn advances a build in 10% steps per material batch delivered, then spends builder-hours (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Construction) — which is already how our hauled-wood projects work, and it is the genre norm rather than an unusual choice.

## 3. Failure modes — what went wrong, was patched, or was regretted

1. **Cohort death waves (Banished).** A baby boom produces a synchronised die-off 60-70 years later; players describe houses emptying into an unrecoverable spiral where imported nomads "make only a minimal dent" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/3044982312814656263/). The mechanical root cause is two step functions in series: births are gated on a **binary** condition (a free house), so births arrive in bursts, and death is a **hard age threshold**, so deaths arrive in matching bursts. Two shipped fixes exist — RimWorld removed death-by-old-age entirely and lets frailty plus illness do the work (https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/4356745862493486100/), and Timberborn randomises lifespan by ±10% (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Life_Expectancy). **Any generational sim with a fixed death age and a gated birth rate will reproduce this bug.**
2. **Unrecoverable spirals bore players rather than challenge them.** On DF tantrum spirals: "the player may be left unable to take any action to salvage the situation, and such a fort's downfall is often from the player abandoning after getting bored staring helplessly at a screen for months" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.34:Tantrum). The design rule from a postmortem-style analysis: "The key to a successful simulation game, therefore, lies in striking a balance between allowing dramatic spirals to occur and providing players with opportunities to recover and redeem themselves" (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-art-of-the-spiral-failure-cascades-in-simulation-games). RimWorld's three named mitigations are worth copying wholesale: raids scale to colony wealth, **expectations adjust downward after losses so survivors need less to stay stable**, and a one-time scripted rescue exists for hopeless states (same source).
3. **Full autonomy with no override reads as unfairness, not difficulty.** Timberborn players report beavers dying of thirst next to available water, including pump operators who will not drink at their own pump (https://timberborn.featureupvote.com/suggestions/344757/thirsty-pumpers-problem-pump-beavers-drink-first-at-their-own-pump-survival-ai); the two top-voted requests are for individual beaver selection and manual assignment (https://timberborn.featureupvote.com/suggestions/202922/better-way-to-add-and-manage-workers-individual-beavers-select-home-and-job); and a "teleport stranded beavers to the District Center" failsafe shipped for years before being removed in February 2026 (https://steamcommunity.com/games/1062090/announcements/detail/523116750517043217). **This is the sharpest warning for a zero-player design**: the closer the player is to zero control, the more the simulation must visibly explain its own bad decisions.
4. **Binary debuff thresholds are the single most-cited balance complaint.** Timberborn's −50% work / −25% movement flip on at a threshold instead of ramping, and the standing request is for gradual penalties (https://timberborn.featureupvote.com/suggestions/330834/scaling-hunger-and-dehydration-debuffs-gradual-penalties). Players describe the resulting notification stream as "the game feels punishing for no reason" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1062090/discussions/0/591763911730045939/).
5. **A mandatory third-party tool is a design smell — but auto-assignment can over-correct.** DF required Dwarf Therapist for years; the community verdict was that per-dwarf micromanagement "was ever necessary was a total travesty", and Tarn Adams leaned toward autolabor-style automation over a spreadsheet (https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3716062978749047381/). The Steam release then over-corrected: default Work Details put unskilled dwarves in every workshop and players complained they "have to jump through hoops just to stop literally everyone from helping in workshops while having no skills whatsoever" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3728448512599510354/).
6. **Moral judgment adjudicated at the end is the most-hated thing in Frostpunk.** The "you crossed the line" epilogue fires on threshold-crossing rather than on decision history; players report it "brings down the whole game" and "doesn't reflect the choices you make" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/3307213006840936501/, https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1749022370115628326/). Contrast the Book of Laws, which is well-liked because its irreversibility is **mechanical and legible up front** — signed laws cannot be unsigned and carry an 18-hour cooldown (https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Book_of_Laws).
7. **A transgressive option that is also mechanically weak reads as the designer pre-judging the player.** Frostpunk's Child Labour is widely called "a trap": the labour gained is small relative to the Hope cost and the scripted injury/death arc (https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696046342853631512/).
8. **Aging speed is the most common immersion complaint and the most common mod target.** Banished's 4-5x aging spawned a mod called "one year is one year" whose entire purpose is removing it (https://banishedinfo.com/mods/view/27-one-year-is-one-year-Update-26-17091434); Norland players repeatedly ask for an aging-speed slider (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1857090/discussions/1/573793962962268326/). **If aging must be compressed, hide the calendar rather than showing the player a contradiction.**

## 4. Concrete proposals for our design

Ordered by how much they change the design. Each names the source mechanic, the mapping onto our nested clocks / needs / voting, and the conflict it creates.

1. **Make "focus" the single currency for every mismatch penalty.** *Source*: Dwarf Fortress — focus is the ratio of met to unmet need values, banded (≥140% very focused, ≤60% badly distracted), and it applies "an effective-skill bonus of up to 50%... a badly distracted dwarf can receive an effective skill penalty of up to 50%" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Need). *Mapping*: needs tick on the hour clock, focus recomputes on the day clock, and `effective_stat = base_stat × focus_mult × trade_fit_mult` feeds output, accident chance and construction quality alike. *Conflict*: our plan lists four parallel debuffs (output, fatigue, accidents, discontent). DF's lesson is to collapse them into one multiplier with four *consequences*, otherwise a cold hungry mismatched worker gets penalised four times and the colony dies faster than anyone can read why. Also note DF deliberately keeps focus **independent of happiness** — keep our discontent separate, because discontent drives the vote and focus drives output, and fusing them makes the vote a referendum on productivity.
2. **Never print a number for a hidden stat; print a bucketed adjective relative to the colony median.** *Source*: Dwarf Fortress attribute descriptions shift at ±250/500/750/1000 from the **species** median, so the same word maps to different numbers in different populations (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Attribute). *Mapping*: strength/wits/hardiness roll on a fixed range; the ASCII citizen card prints one word per stat. *Conflict*: this directly fights "stats are only revealed through noisy performance" — an adjective on the card *is* the reveal. Resolution: print the adjective only for stats the colony has **observed**, i.e. after N worker-days in a trade that exercises that stat, and let the displayed word be the mayor's running estimate, which starts wrong and converges. W&R already ships the gated version of this: loyalty is hidden until the Secret Police install spy equipment in the home (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens).
3. **Give the mayor exactly one economic lever, not a job-assignment UI.** *Source*: Ostriv — the player sets a town-wide basic wage, a managers' wage, a laborers' wage, and a **per-building wage percentage**, and citizens then choose for themselves; the developer's stated reason for refusing direct assignment is *"you can't just assign someone to work in specific place and live with specific spouse because, you know, that's mathematically effective"* (https://steamcommunity.com/app/773790/discussions/0/2268069450224718072/?ctp=2, https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Town_hall). *Mapping*: this is our "indirect influence through a mayor" made concrete and cheap — one scalar per building that biases the job-scoring function. *Conflict*: Ostriv's lever is wages, and we have no money. Substitute the scarce thing we do have: **firewood ration** or **hearth priority** per workplace. Careful — Ostriv shows the failure mode too: set the laborers' wage too low and construction starves because everyone prefers a vacant regular slot (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Economy).
4. **Score jobs, don't just take the nearest — and weight prior experience into the score.** *Source*: Ostriv's `checkForBetterJob` weighs **distance, prior experience with that job type, and wage**, with a hard distance cut-off added later after complaints (same source); Shining Rock moved the same direction for his next game, scoring actions and then picking "one of the top ones" rather than the single best (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2021-02-16-designing-the-ai/). *Mapping*: a vacancy is filled by `score = w_dist·distance + w_exp·experience_in_trade + w_lever·mayor_bias`, and we sample from the top few rather than taking argmax, which injects variety for free. *Conflict*: our stated rule is "nearest idle citizen takes a vacancy". Scoring is strictly better and still produces mismatches — because experience is weighted, not required, and because the distance term is the wrong distance (see 5).
5. **Use a deliberately cheap, deliberately wrong distance metric, and split the radius rule by job class.** *Source*: DF picks the tile with "minimum distance (in the maximum norm) from the current position, **regardless of how long the path to actually get there is**" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Labor); Banished used straight-line distance and the developer named it as the flaw (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-11-21-more-bugs-pathfinding-problems/); Banished 1.0.3 then split the rule — "laborers don't have any constraints on when they'll go to do work or how far away it is", while other professions "stick closer to their home and job unless a job is fairly old and no laborer has done it" (https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2014-05-31-laborer-update/); W&R caps the daily job search at a **~450 m walking radius** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). *Mapping*: haulers feeding construction get an unbounded radius; trade workers get a bounded one, with an ageing-job escape valve. *Conflict*: none with our design — this is the cheapest mismatch generator available. But it must be **narrated**, or players read it as a pathfinding bug, exactly as Banished's players did.
6. **Kill death-by-fixed-age. Use accumulating frailty plus a randomised lifespan.** *Source*: RimWorld has **no death-by-old-age event at all** — elderly pawns accumulate health conditions and lose immunity-gain speed until an illness kills them (https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/4356745862493486100/); Timberborn randomises lifespan by **±10%** (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Life_Expectancy). *Mapping*: hardiness decays with age on the year clock; each cold snap rolls against current hardiness. *Conflict*: nothing in our design, and it fixes the worst bug we would otherwise inherit — Banished's cohort death wave, where a housing-gated baby boom produces a synchronised die-off 60-70 years later (https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/3044982312814656263/). It also makes **winter the agent of death**, which is thematically what a Frostpunk-flavoured game wants.
7. **Make profession a property of the household, not the individual.** *Source*: Lords & Villeins — "The profession of a family determined what job they can do. This is determined by the game and cannot be removed", and a spouse marrying in "will learn the family's profession" (https://wiki.lordsandvilleins.com/subdom/wiki/index.php?title=Professions); Ostriv has stored **per-citizen experience for every type of work** since 2017 (https://ostrivgame.com/status-report-and-closed-testing-announcement/); Norland gives heirs **double lesson XP until age 21** (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Norland/Population). *Mapping*: our "children inherit a parent's trade" becomes structural rather than a probability roll, and scarcity bites hard — a new smith requires a smith's child to survive, or a bad-fit assignment. *Conflict*: this collides head-on with needs-driven voting. If citizens vote for a building no household can staff, the mismatch is permanent. Keep Banished's and Ostriv's escape valve: an unassigned **laborer pool** that absorbs anything nobody's trade covers, with Ostriv's guidance of holding **20-25% of the workforce unemployed** as reserve (https://ostriv.fandom.com/wiki/Laborers).
8. **Split job fit into two axes that point at different buildings.** *Source*: Against the Storm — **Proficiency** gives a 10% chance of double output in one tagged set, **Comfort** gives **+5 Resolve** in a *different* tagged set (https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Against_the_Storm/Humans). *Mapping*: hidden stats (strength/wits/hardiness) determine output fit; a separate, also-hidden temperament determines whether the citizen is content in that trade, feeding discontent and therefore the vote. *Conflict*: adds a fourth hidden stat and more simulation surface. The payoff is that the mayor's best-output assignment is rarely the one citizens would vote for, which is the entire dramatic engine of a zero-player game — without it, optimal and popular converge and there is nothing to watch.
9. **Ramp debuffs continuously, then amplify below a floor.** *Source*: Timberborn's binary threshold flips (−50% work when Starving, −25% movement when Thirsty) are its **single most-cited balance complaint**, with a standing request for gradual penalties (https://timberborn.featureupvote.com/suggestions/330834/scaling-hunger-and-dehydration-debuffs-gradual-penalties); W&R ships the better hybrid — unmet demand costs happiness on every roll with an **amplified penalty once the need drops below 5%** (https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Citizens). *Mapping*: warmth/rest/hunger each contribute a continuous term to focus, with a knee near zero. *Conflict*: continuous penalties are harder to read in ASCII than a status word. Print the band name (DF's "Distracted", "Badly distracted") while computing the continuous value underneath.
10. **Let the age pyramid be an output, and make it the main ASCII readout.** *Source*: Timberborn — high well-being shortens childhood and lengthens adult life, so kits fall from **12.00% to 3.12% of population** without the player setting any birth rate (https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Kits). *Mapping*: warmth and food surplus modulate maturation rate on the year clock; the terminal shows an age histogram. *Conflict*: our births are housing-gated like Banished's, which is a step function and produces bursts. Either smooth the gate into a per-couple seasonal probability scaled by spare housing, or keep the hard gate and rely on proposal 6's randomised lifespan to desynchronise the deaths. Do at least one of the two.
11. **Reassignment must cost something, or "nobody quits on their own" is arbitrary.** *Source*: RimWorld drains XP continuously above skill level 10 (https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Skills); DF rusts unused skills and destroys "50% of the gained experience points (rounded down, plus 1)" when retraining a rusty one (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Skill); ONI inverts it — "Learning a Skill will increase the Duplicant's total morale need by the Skill's numeric tier", reduced by 1 if the dupe is interested in that work (https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Skills). *Mapping*: trade experience decays on the season clock above a threshold, so a citizen moved off their trade loses standing and the colony pays for churn. *Conflict*: this makes recovery from a bad assignment slower, which compounds spiral risk — so pair it with RimWorld's expectations mechanic (proposal 12) rather than shipping it alone.
12. **Ship an anti-spiral mechanic before shipping the spiral.** *Source*: DF's tantrum spiral is the genre's cautionary tale — "the player may be left unable to take any action to salvage the situation, and such a fort's downfall is often from the player abandoning after getting bored" (https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.34:Tantrum). RimWorld's named mitigations: threats scale to colony wealth, **expectations adjust downward after losses so survivors need less to stay stable**, and a one-time scripted rescue exists for hopeless states (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-art-of-the-spiral-failure-cascades-in-simulation-games). *Mapping*: after a mass-casualty winter, drop the baseline need thresholds for survivors for N seasons. *Conflict*: it blunts the tragedy our theme is built on. But in a **zero-player** game the risk is strictly worse than in RimWorld — the player cannot intervene at all, so an unrecoverable spiral is not a hard-won loss, it is several minutes of watching a spreadsheet die. This is the most important proposal in the list.
13. **If we add mayor decrees, make them irreversible, cheap to read, and judged immediately — never at the end.** *Source*: Frostpunk's Book of Laws works because a signed law "cannot be unsigned" and carries an **18-hour in-game cooldown** (https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Book_of_Laws), whereas its "you crossed the line" epilogue is the most-criticised thing in the game precisely because it fires on threshold-crossing rather than decision history — players report it "brings down the whole game" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/3307213006840936501/). *Mapping*: one decree per season, permanent, with its mechanical cost stated up front. *Conflict*: a decree is a player action, and we are zero-player. Resolution: the decree is a **standing policy the mayor interprets**, not an order — the player sets the policy, the mayor's own stats determine how well it is executed. Also avoid Frostpunk's other trap: never make the transgressive option mechanically weak, or players read the design as pre-judging them (https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696046342853631512/).

## 5. What I could not source

- **Banished's real-time year length at 1x** is community-estimated only and the estimates do not reconcile (~25 min at 1x in one thread, ~10 min at 10x in another); Shining Rock never published a figure. Likewise Banished's exact old-age death distribution — the "max 75 with flex of 15" figure circulates but I could not find it in a primary source, and the two community sources disagree on whether aging runs at 4x or 5x.
- **Frostpunk's day length in real minutes** — no authoritative figure found; player estimates range 10-30 minutes.
- **Crusader Kings 3 lifecycle numbers** from a designer source — only the structural point (you play a dynasty, not a character) is sourced.
- **W&R's official wiki** (wiki.hoodedhorse.com) sits behind Cloudflare and was unreachable, so all W&R claims come from the Fandom wiki and Steam threads. Related trap worth repeating: the widely circulated "uneducated worker = 50% penalty" number is **a forum design proposal, not a mechanic**.
- **Attribute inheritance from parents in Dwarf Fortress** — the wiki's attribute page says nothing about it, and I found no primary source either way, so I cannot confirm DF models heritability at all.
