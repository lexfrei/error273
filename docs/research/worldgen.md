# An unbounded world, expeditions, and citizens a colony raises — research notes

Scope: (a) generating a world with no edge — lazy deterministic chunks, sparse storage, gradient noise, biome assignment, and the traps that come with distance from the origin; (b) when a forager turns for home, what a built heat bubble costs, and what a colony does about a death it did not see; (c) how a stat can pass culturally rather than genetically, and what the evidence says about how much of a person an upbringing decides. Every mechanic claim below carries a link. Sentences marked "(from memory, unverified)" have no source and should be treated as a hypothesis.

## 1. Per-source findings

### (a) A world with no edge: chunks, noise, biomes, and distance from the origin

**Minecraft** is the reference implementation and the interesting parts are the ones that did not work first time. A Java Edition chunk is a 16x16 column spanning 384 blocks vertically since the 1.18 Caves & Cliffs update — Y -64 to Y 319, against Y 0-256 before it — which is 98,304 blocks in a full column ([Chunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk)). Chunks are stored 32x32 to a region file in the Anvil format, named `r.x.z.mca` from the chunk coordinates floor-divided by 32 ([Region file format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Region_file_format)). The point worth correcting, because it is widely misremembered: a generated chunk is **written to disk as NBT and reloaded from there**, not recomputed from the seed on each visit. Seed-plus-coordinate determinism is real and is what external pre-generation tools rely on, but it is not the live game's persistence path, because block edits, entities and tile data would be lost ([Chunk format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk_format)).

The world is not actually infinite either. The default border sits at X/Z of plus or minus **29,999,984** — one chunk short of the plus or minus 30,000,000 engine limit, placed there deliberately so players cannot fall through the world at the true edge ([World border](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_border), [MC-70076](https://bugs-legacy.mojang.com/browse/MC-70076)).

**The Far Lands are the canonical precision failure and the numbers are specific.** They began around X/Z of plus or minus **12,550,821**, with the overflow at about 12,550,824, because the terrain-height noise generator's cumulative value grew by roughly **171.103 per block** until it exceeded the available precision; the fix, in Beta 1.8, was a **modulo 2^24 wraparound on the noise coordinates** ([Far Lands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Far_Lands)). That is the same 2^24 that bounds exactly representable integers in an f32, and it is the number our own `as_vec2()` distance computations live under.

**Cascading worldgen is the canonical order-dependence failure.** Chunk decoration — trees, ore veins, structures — historically wrote features across chunk borders into neighbours that had not been generated, forcing those to generate too and potentially cascading further; the community named the bug class "cascading worldgen lag". Modern generation staggers each chunk through discrete per-chunk statuses (`empty` → `structure_starts` → ... → `features` → `full`) precisely to bound how much of a neighbour has to exist before it can be touched ([Chunk format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk_format), [the hunt for cascading worldgen lag](https://www.minecraftforum.net/forums/mapping-and-modding-java-edition/minecraft-mods/modification-development/2882830-the-hunt-for-cascading-worldgen-lag)).

**Gradient noise is position-independent, and that is the property lazy chunking is built on.** Perlin's original is "An Image Synthesizer", *ACM SIGGRAPH Computer Graphics* 19(3):287-296, 1985 ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/325165.325247)), refined in "Improving Noise", *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 21(3):681-682, 2002 ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/566654.566636)). The relevant distinction is that **value noise** assigns a random value at each lattice point and interpolates it, while **gradient noise** assigns a random gradient vector, takes dot products with the offsets from the surrounding lattice corners, and interpolates those — which is why gradient noise passes through zero at every node and looks the way it does. Either kind can be evaluated at any coordinate with no prior state, at a cost of 2^n corner evaluations in n dimensions. OpenSimplex exists because simplex noise was patented (US 6,867,776, 2001, for 3D and higher); Kurt Spencer wrote it in 2014 to route around that, using a stretched hypercubic honeycomb and a larger kernel, which is smoother and slower ([OpenSimplex noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSimplex_noise)).

**Biomes are a lookup in a parameter space, and the games use more axes than the science.** Whittaker's diagram is the science: *Communities and Ecosystems*, 2nd edition, Macmillan 1975, plots terrestrial biomes on **two** axes, mean annual temperature and mean annual precipitation, with each biome occupying a bounded region of that plane ([Robert Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Whittaker_(ecologist))). Minecraft 1.18 and later looks each column up in a multi-dimensional noise-parameter space — **continentalness, erosion, temperature, humidity, weirdness** (which drives peaks-and-valleys) and **depth** — through the generator's noise router ([Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)). Dwarf Fortress seeds **elevation, rainfall, temperature, drainage, volcanism and wildness** on a variable-size grid, fills fractionally between the seed points, determines vegetation from elevation, rainfall and temperature together, then adjusts rainfall for rain shadows and recomputes temperature from elevation, rainfall and forest cover; its worlds run 17x17 (Pocket) to 257x257 region tiles, each region tile is 16x16 local blocks, and each local block is 48x48 tiles ([World generation](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation)).

**Nobody in the genre ships an unbounded playfield; they ship a bounded playfield under a coarse world.** Dwarf Fortress's embark is one local area under the region map above. RimWorld separates a hex-tiled planet-scale world map, which caravans travel between settlements on, from the local colony map, a fixed square grid of 250x250 cells by default. Songs of Syx's settlement map is a fixed square grid, 768x768 by default with a mod raising it to 1024x1024, under a separate world map whose other settlements are reached only through caravans and trade depots ([World](https://songsofsyx.com/wiki/index.php/World), [larger-map mod](https://mod.io/g/songsofsyx/m/larger-map)). Caves of Qud is finite and its structure is precise: the world map is **80 parasangs wide by 25 tall**, each parasang is a 3x3 block of zones, and each zone is an 80x25 cell grid, so one parasang is 240x75 = 18,000 cells ([Terminology](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Terminology)). Terraria is the clean contrast case — worlds are finite fixed-size rectangles at Small 4200x1200, Medium 6400x1800 and Large 8400x2400 tiles, so Large is double Small in each dimension and four times the area ([World size](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/World_size)).

**Pathfinding over an open world is a hierarchy problem, and flow fields need a bounded region.** Botea, Müller and Schaeffer's `HPA*` — "Near Optimal Hierarchical Path-Finding", *Journal of Game Development* 1(1):7-28, 2004 — abstracts a grid map into clusters with entrances, searches the abstract graph, and refines: about **10x faster** than a highly optimised `A*` on grid-based game maps for paths within roughly **1%** of optimal length ([abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228785110_Near_optimal_hierarchical_path-finding_HPA)). Emerson's "Crowd Pathfinding and Steering Using Flow Field Tiles", *Game AI Pro* chapter 23, developed for *Supreme Commander 2*, computes a field toward one goal **once over a bounded tile region** and shares it across every agent heading there, replacing per-agent recomputation ([chapter PDF](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter23_Crowd_Pathfinding_and_Steering_Using_Flow_Field_Tiles.pdf)). The bounded region is the load-bearing word: a flow field over an unbounded world is not a technique, it is a memory leak.

**Floating origin is the standard answer to precision, and its numbers are the ones that matter.** The camera is redefined as the coordinate origin and the world moves around it, pioneered by Christopher G. Thorne in 2005; accuracy **halves for every power of two units away from the origin**, 32-bit single precision cannot represent fractions past **8,388,608** units, and the visible artefact is spatial jitter as vertices snap to representable values ([Floating origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_origin)). Kerbal Space Program's Deep Space Kraken is the shipped instance: far from the origin, float rounding made a vessel's parts jitter, separate and explode for no visible reason, and the fix (Krakensbane) makes the active vessel the origin and moves the universe around it ([Deep Space Kraken](https://kerbalspaceprogram.fandom.com/wiki/Deep_Space_Kraken), [Kraken Drive](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/Kraken_Drive)).

The academic background, for the parts none of the wikis cover: Shaker, Togelius and Nelson, *Procedural Content Generation in Games* (Springer, 2016), with author-version chapters freely hosted ([pcgbook.com](https://www.pcgbook.com/)), and Smelik, Tutenel, Bidarra and Beneš, "A Survey on Procedural Modelling for Virtual Worlds", *Computer Graphics Forum* 33(6):31-50, 2014 ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.12276)).

### (b) When to turn for home: foraging theory, and the games that built a leash

Charnov's **marginal value theorem** is the whole of the patch-leaving question and the paper is open access ([Charnov 1976, *Theoretical Population Biology* 9(2):129-136](https://digitalrepository.unm.edu/biol_fsp/9/), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/004058097690040X)). Its own wording, from page 132: "The predator should leave the patch it is presently in when the marginal capture rate in the patch drops to the average capture rate for the habitat." The average is over the whole cycle including travel: net rate is `(E_e - t * E_T) / T_u`, with `t` the interpatch travel time, `E_T` the energetic cost per unit travel time and `T_u` the total time per patch-use cycle. The result that matters here is the counterintuitive one — **longer travel means staying longer**, not less long. Travel enters the cycle as dead time contributing no gain, so lengthening it lowers the achievable habitat-wide rate; a lower rate is a shallower tangent, and a shallower line sweeping up against a decelerating gain curve touches it later ([Wikipedia: Marginal value theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_value_theorem): "Individuals will stay longer if (1) patches are farther apart or (2) current patches are poor in resources"). One citation trap worth naming: Charnov's own figure draws the tangent ray from the plain time-axis origin, while the textbook version shifts the origin left by the travel time so the tangent's slope reads directly as the habitat average. The two are equivalent, but "the MVT graph" means different pictures in different sources. Empirical support inside the paper itself is Krebs, Ryan and Charnov's 1974 chickadee study, whose predictions of a constant giving-up time within an environment and a lower one in richer environments "were both supported by the data"; Cowie's great tits, where travel time was lengthened by making dish lids harder to open and patch residence rose ([*Nature* 268:137-139](https://www.nature.com/articles/268137a0)); and Kacelnik's starlings over 1,364 provisioning trips at feeders 8-600 m from the nest ([*Journal of Animal Ecology* 53(1):283-299](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76c70bd129ca384f040a2e005ea7c623f6ecbea3)).

**Central place foraging** is the variant where the forager must carry the load home, which is exactly a hauler ([Orians & Pearson 1979, in *Analysis of Ecological Systems*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_place_foraging)). The prediction: load size rises with distance from the central place, because a fixed round-trip cost is amortised over the load, so a bigger load from a farther patch spreads that cost over more resource ([Wetterer 1989, *Theoretical Population Biology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0040580989900348), which extends the model to the case where the load itself slows the return). Documented instances: red-winged blackbirds bringing many small prey per trip where search is cheap and single large insects where it is expensive; honeybees increasing nectar load with travel time to the site; beavers preferentially felling larger-diameter trees the farther they are from the lodge. The evidence is not unanimous — at least one study found no significant load-size-by-distance interaction — so this is a strong tendency rather than a law.

**The energy budget rule** is the sourced form of "a starving forager gambles", and the direction is worth stating carefully because it is easy to invert. A forager on a **negative** energy budget — expected intake below what it needs to clear the period — is **risk-prone**, preferring the variable option; a forager on a **positive** budget is **risk-averse**, preferring the constant one of equal mean. Caraco, Martindale and Whittam ran it on seven yellow-eyed juncos with a constant perch always covering the 24-hour energy cost against a variable perch of equal mean, and two birds individually retested **switched preference** when moved from surplus to deficit ([*Animal Behaviour* 28(3):820-830](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347280801424)). Stephens formalised it as minimising the probability of falling short of a critical survival threshold, which is risk aversion above the threshold and risk proneness below it ([*Animal Behaviour* 29(2):628-629](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232573336_The_logic_of_risk-sensitive_foraging_preferences), [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-sensitive_foraging_models)). The mechanism is the shape of the fitness curve: concave once needs are met, so variance only hurts; threshold-shaped near a survival cutoff, so variance is the only thing that can carry a forager over a bar its mean cannot reach. The caveat is large and recent — Houston's critical review states flatly that "the data do not match this prediction", citing a review in which only **10 of 35** studies confirmed the predicted switch ([*Biological Reviews* 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/brv.13031)). Use it as a stylised rule, not as biology.

**Lévy flight foraging** is the search-pattern half and it comes with an unusually clean controversy. Viswanathan et al. showed that for a forager looking for sparse, randomly located, revisitable targets it can only detect nearby, a power-law step-length distribution with exponent about 2 is the optimal search ([*Nature* 401:911-914](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10553906/)). Then Edwards et al. re-analysed the wandering-albatross data that carried most of the empirical weight and found no Lévy behaviour at all: the flight-time distribution fit a gamma with an exponential tail, and the power-law tail was a measurement artefact — the wet/dry foot sensors had recorded birds sitting dry on land or on the nest as extremely long flights ([*Nature* 449:1044-1047](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06199), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17960243/)). The same reanalysis found no support in the deer or bumblebee data either. Sims et al. then reported over a million displacement records across sharks, bony fish, sea turtles and penguins landing close to the theoretical optimum ([*Nature* 451:1098](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18305542/)) and published a direct reply saying their conclusions "were not overturned in any part" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2288)). The mathematics of the optimum is uncontested; whether animals do it is an open argument.

**Giving-up density** is the measurable version of a patch-leaving rule ([Brown 1988, *Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* 22:37-47](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00395696)). GUD is the density of resource left in a patch at the moment a forager quits it, measured with seed trays — a known quantity of food mixed into an inedible substrate, weighed for leftovers. The optimal GUD is where harvest rate balances the sum of three costs: metabolic, predation, and the missed opportunity of not doing something else, `H = C + P + MOC` ([Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2021.718887/full), [a practical guide](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-013-1609-3)). A higher observed GUD means the forager priced the patch as more dangerous or the alternatives as better, which is why GUD is used as a bioassay for a landscape of fear. Binford's forager/collector distinction supplies the human version: foragers practise residential mobility and move the group to the resource, collectors practise logistical mobility and send task groups out from a fixed base to bring resources back — which is the pattern a colony has ([Binford 1980, *American Antiquity* 45(1):4-20](https://www.jstor.org/stable/280065)).

**Don't Starve** is the shipped leash. Charlie attacks only in complete darkness with no light source, the first hit landing 5-10 seconds after total darkness with an audio warning during the window, dealing **100 health plus 20 sanity** per hit — armour absorbs only the health half — repeating every 5-11 seconds until the player dies or makes light ([Charlie/Darkness](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Charlie/Darkness)). Sanity is the slower leash and its rates are published: -5/min at dusk, at night or in caves, up to -50/min in total darkness, up to -3.3/min from rain, up to -40/min near monsters, up to -400/min near a major boss; regeneration comes from a tent or lean-to at +60/min or a friendly Pig's proximity aura at +6.25/min ([Sanity](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Sanity)). Crossing thresholds converts atmosphere into damage: eyes in the dark at 60%, passive Crawling Horrors at 50%, aggressive red tendrils at 15%, Terrorbeaks at 10%, and a Crawling Horror that has turned hostile below 15% deals 20 damage every 2.5 seconds and carries its own -100 sanity/min aura ([Crawling Horror](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Crawling_Horror)). The camp side is priced too: a Campfire is 2 Logs and 3 Cut Grass and burns through four discrete stages of 2/3/4/5 tile radius for up to 4.5 minutes, losing fuel up to 250% faster in rain; upgrading to a Fire Pit costs 2 Logs and 12 Rocks over the existing campfire, keeps the same four stages, doubles maximum burn to 6 minutes and resists rain ([Campfire](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Campfire), [Fire Pit](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Fire_Pit)).

**Frostpunk 1** is the closest tonal analogue and the only source with published numbers for a heat bubble you build. The Generator's steam level sets temperature bonus and coal cost together: level 1 is +1 degree at 6 coal an hour, level 4 is +4 at 24 an hour or 576 a day, and it heats "in its immediate area in a radial fashion" with buildings needing a road connection to receive it ([Generator](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Generator)). The temperature scale is banded — Comfortable at 0 and above, then Livable, Chilly, Cold, Very Cold and Freezing at -41 or below, with moderate illness risk from Cold, high illness plus low amputation risk at Very Cold, and transitions between adjacent bands taking 12 hours ([Heat](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Heat)). The **Steam Hub is the range extender**: 20 Steel to build, one road tile, 3 coal an hour at range 1 and 6 at range 2 ([Steam Hub](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Steam_Hub)). And its upgrade is the single most useful number in this report — Range Upgrade II costs 30 Wood and 20 Steel, extends range by **+50%**, which the wiki itself computes as **225% of the original heated area**, while **doubling** coal cost, and the same page concedes this nets "about +10% increase in efficiency", so the upgrade is bought to consolidate hubs rather than to save coal ([Range Upgrade II](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Steam_Hub_Range_Upgrade_II_(Technology))). Scouts cost 40 Wood and 5 Workers per team, are gated behind a Beacon, and start at a limit of one team researchable to three ([Scouts](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Scouts)); Outposts are built from a 25 Wood and 45 Steel depot and send periodic caravans home ([Frostlands](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Frostlands)).

**RimWorld caravans** are the cautionary tale rather than the model. Speed is `(13.6 * mount multiplier * mass ratio) / terrain difficulty`, where the mass ratio runs from about 200% empty to about 50% at full capacity and terrain difficulty is 1.0 for flat temperate forest against 4.0 for swamp and 4.0-5.0 for mountains, with carrying capacity at body size times 35 kg ([Caravan](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Caravan)). Travellers eat 1.6 nutrition a day from the soonest-expiring allowed food, and the documented trap is that colonists ignore food not on their allowed list while starving mid-trip; malnutrition then accumulates at roughly 1.51-2.27% severity an hour through six named stages, wounds stop healing at any stage, and severity 1.0 kills ([Malnutrition](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Malnutrition)). Players describe caravan packing as "one of the most painful experiences in the game", with colonists neglecting their own needs and pack animals collapsing during loading, and name the asymmetry that loading grabs many items per trip while unloading takes one ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3058492678373896284)); the recurring complaint is that the cost of a run exceeds the reward ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3058491773343082052/?ctp=4)).

**Kenshi** is the source for a soft failure at distance. A character whose vital part drops below their KO point — `10 + Toughness^0.75`, running from about -10 to -85 across the stat range — enters a recovery coma rather than dying, and wakes when vital health rises back above zero ([Guide to Health](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Guide_to_Health), [Statistics](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Statistics)). Leg damage cuts run speed rather than a skill, and full-speed running needs at least 55.6% leg health ([Damage](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Damage)). What happens next is the design: slavers and manhunters periodically restock by picking unconscious characters up off the ground, and a squadmate can equally pick up an unconscious ally and carry them home ([Slave Capture](https://kenshi.fandom.com/wiki/Slave_Capture)). Encumbrance taxes the food clock directly at +0.007 hunger drain per 1% load, so a fully loaded traveller burns food about twice as fast as one travelling light.

**Dwarf Fortress** is the source for what a colony does about a death it did not see. Corpse hauling is a dedicated burial labour, and the wiki warns against assigning it to "important or depression-prone dwarves" because haulers meet the trauma, the wildlife and the invaders on the way ([Hauling](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Hauling)). An unburied corpse generates unhappy thoughts in family and friends and can raise a vengeful ghost, and rotting corpses produce miasma that gives unhappy thoughts to anyone walking through it ([Corpse](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Corpse)). Where the body cannot be recovered, an engraved **memorial slab** calms or dispels the ghost without the corpse, and reading it in game gives the dead unit's arrival and death information or, if ambiguous, the killer's identity ([Slab](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Slab)). A unit gets a **missing** status the instant it leaves the map, tracked on the Dead/Missing list before the formal announcement fires. Caves of Qud is the confirmed negative case: its death screen records cause of death, turns survived, and the last 30 log lines, but there is no missing-person, search or memorial mechanic on either the death or the corpse page ([Death](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Death), [Corpse](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Corpse)).

### (c) Cultural transmission, skill formation, and what upbringing can carry

Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman's *Cultural Transmission and Evolution: A Quantitative Approach* (Princeton, 1981) is the founding formal treatment and it supplies the vocabulary this chapter needs ([Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691082837/cultural-transmission-and-evolution)). Three pathways: **vertical**, parent to child; **oblique**, a non-parental adult of the older generation to a naive individual — a teacher, an elder, the trade standing next door; **horizontal**, peer to peer inside one generation. A 2024 retrospective quotes the original framing directly — "the first cultural transmission pathways to be modeled involved vertical transmission... and random oblique transmission... from randomly chosen, and possibly unrelated, group members to naive individuals" — and identifies the balance between vertical and oblique transmission as what drives trait variation ([Half a century of quantitative cultural evolution, PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11621465/)). The dynamics differ by pathway and that difference is the design content: purely vertical transmission is the slowest channel available and paces change at a generation, while oblique and horizontal transmission are faster, and adaptation to a changing environment is slow under purely inter-generational transmission but fast once intra-generational transmission lets an outdated behaviour be replaced inside one generation ([JASSS review](https://www.jasss.org/9/1/9.html)). Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman modelled the horizontal channel as an epidemic outright: individuals who did not acquire a trait vertically contact those who did and convert at a horizontal transmission rate, which is the same shape as an infection rate ([conformity as an SIS model, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040580913000907)). That is the sourced justification for our tradition cards already being an SIR process, and it says the household and the card are not two competing systems but the vertical and the horizontal arm of one.

Boyd and Richerson's *Culture and the Evolutionary Process* (Chicago, 1985) is the founding text of **dual inheritance theory**: genes and culture are two parallel, partially independent inheritance systems that coevolve ([press.uchicago.edu](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo5970597.html), [Population thinking and natural selection in dual-inheritance theory, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3326234/), [Henrich, Dual Inheritance Theory](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~henrich/pdfs/Ch38%20-%20Dual%20Inheritance%20Theory.pdf)). Their biased-transmission taxonomy names the three ways a variant can be favoured while it spreads: **content bias**, where the variant wins on its own properties; **frequency-dependent bias**, whose conformist pole copies the majority variant more than in proportion to its frequency and whose anti-conformist pole does the reverse ([Cultural evolution of conformity and anticonformity, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2004102117)); and **model-based bias**, of which **prestige bias** is the important case — copying whoever is successful rather than copying on the merits. Henrich and Gil-White's founding treatment of prestige separates it from dominance: dominance is deference extracted by force or resource control, prestige is deference freely conferred by learners who want proximity to a model worth copying ([Henrich & Gil-White 2001, *Evolution and Human Behavior* 22(3):165-196](https://henrich.fas.harvard.edu/publications/evolution-prestige-freely-conferred-deference-mechanism-enhancing-benefits), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11384884/), [review in Nature Humanities & Social Sciences Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-019-0228-7)). These are the rules for *how a card spreads*, and they are the reason a card model does not need a fourth channel to express schooling: a school is a prestige-biased oblique contact.

Behaviour genetics supplies the argument for keeping a random term and says how large it should be. Plomin and Daniels 1987, "Why are children in the same family so different from one another?", argued from twin and adoption data that most environmental variance in personality is **nonshared** — experienced differently by siblings raised together — rather than shared ([30-years-later commentary, *International Journal of Epidemiology*](https://academic.oup.com/ije/article/40/3/582/739694), [Nonshared Environment a Decade Later, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/070674370104600302)). Plomin's own 2024 update is blunter and more useful to us: thirty years of looking for systematic causes of that nonshared variance mostly failed, measured predictors explain roughly 1-2% of the variance of psychopathology outcomes, and the channel behaves like individual stochasticity rather than an engineerable input ([Nonshared environment: Real but random, PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11472802/)). The Wilson effect gives the age shape: genetic variance in IQ runs about 20% at age 5 and rises to roughly 80% by 18-20, while shared-environment variance falls from about 55% at age 5 to approximately zero from age 12 ([Bouchard, *Twin Research and Human Genetics* 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23919982/)). Polderman et al.'s meta-analysis over 17,804 traits and 14,558,903 twin pairs found mean heritability of 49%, with 69% of traits consistent with a purely additive model showing no detectable shared-environment contribution ([*Nature Genetics* 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/ng.3285)). Two qualifications matter for a colony that is often starving. Shared environment does hold up for *achievement* rather than personality — about 15% of variance for reading and 10% for mathematics ([Top 10 Replicated Findings from Behavioral Genetics, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4739500/)) — and the Scarr-Rowe interaction says the split itself moves with resources: in impoverished families shared environment explains roughly 60% of IQ variance with heritability near zero, while in affluent families heritability is high and shared environment negligible. Turkheimer's three laws state the field's consensus compactly, including that a substantial share of variance in complex behavioural traits is explained by neither genes nor shared family environment ([*Current Directions in Psychological Science* 2000](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8721.00084)).

Cunha and Heckman's "The Technology of Skill Formation", *American Economic Review* 97(2):31-47, names the two properties a childhood accumulator needs ([NBER WP 12840](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12840/w12840.pdf), [IZA DP 2550](https://docs.iza.org/dp2550.pdf), [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.97.2.31)). **Self-productivity**: skills acquired in one period persist into the next and raise the productivity of later investment. **Dynamic complementarity**: a unit of investment is worth more when the existing stock is already higher, so investment is a complement to prior skill and not a substitute for missing skill. The stages where investment in a given skill is unusually productive they call **sensitive periods**. The associated Heckman curve — returns to human-capital investment highest in early childhood and falling with age — is worth citing carefully rather than confidently: Rea and Burton find the broader evidence does not support a monotonically declining return with age ([*Journal of Economic Surveys* 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joes.12353?af=R), [working paper](https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/2019-rea.pdf)), and Heckman has since characterised it as an illustrative proposition about an optimal investment portfolio rather than an estimated function ([Gelman's write-up](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2019/04/06/heckman-curve-update-data-dont-seem-support-claim-human-capital-investments-effective-targeted-younger-ages/)). Take the mechanism, not the curve.

Early-life nutrition is the one place where the empirical shape is specific enough to tune against. The Dutch Hunger Winter cohort is the best-controlled human natural experiment available: the birth cohort profile traces 2,414 term singletons from 1994 ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7934722/)), and women exposed to famine before age 1 reached 4.45 cm less adult height, those exposed before age 2 4.08 cm less, than unexposed controls ([*Economics & Human Biology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1570677X1630140X)). The critical window for stunting runs from roughly nine months before birth to twenty-four months after ([*American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23553163/)), but it is not a single irreversible die roll: height catch-up frequently occurs between 24 months and adulthood even without supplementation and adolescence is a second window where growth interventions still work ([Catch-up growth, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7754567/)), while cognitive effects of the early deficit persist more stubbornly than the physical ones ([A4NH](https://a4nh.cgiar.org/2020/09/10/catch-up-growth-cannot-undo-damage-from-undernutrition/)). The Barker hypothesis is the umbrella ([fetal origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_origins_hypothesis), [PubMed review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16441686/)).

Cold specifically is the weakest link in the chain and has to be stated as such. The clearest evidence for exposure-driven rather than inherited cold adaptation is the Korean and Japanese diving women: basal metabolic rate ran about 5% above the Dubois standard in summer against 35% in winter, attributed to repeated cold-water immersion, and the seasonal fluctuation **disappeared** in a 1980-82 study after the divers adopted wetsuits in 1977 ([*Journal of Applied Physiology* 1963, republished](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31090384/), [*Journal of Physiological Anthropology* 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40101-017-0146-6)). Brown adipose tissue is framed by a 2022 review as shaped by a stack of modes including developmental adaptation, with BAT growth particularly sensitive in early childhood ([*Evolutionary Anthropology*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/evan.21930)), and habitual cold exposure shows measurable BAT differences in Arctic adults ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355669/), [*JCEM*](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/111/5/e1331/8324870)). The honest reading is that human cold adaptation is exposure-driven and **reverses when the exposure stops**, which is evidence for an acclimatisation term, not for a hardiness stat locked at fifteen.

The games converge on one shape. Crusader Kings 3 runs education from about age 6 to 16 under an assigned guardian, accumulating a hidden score whose tier depends on the guardian's own relevant skill plus half their Learning, shifted by the guardian's intelligence traits, with a penalty for a focus that contradicts the child's inherited childhood trait; five tracks with four tiers each, roughly +2 to the relevant skill and +10% lifestyle experience per tier, and childhood personality traits picked at discrete events around ages 3, 9, 11 and 13 ([CK3 wiki: Attributes](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Attributes), [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2230804215)). Two credible sources disagree on the exact tier thresholds, which is a patch-version artefact rather than an error, so the thresholds are not a number to copy. CK2's Conclave version is structurally the same: a childhood focus at 6-11 and an adolescent focus at 12-15, both locked once chosen, with the guardian's attributes shaping the outcome ([CK2 wiki](https://ck2.paradoxwikis.com/Education_(Conclave))). RimWorld's Biotech replaces backstories for player-raised children with **growth moments** at ages 7, 10 and 13 after a baby becomes a child at 3, each granting a work-type unlock plus a choice of trait and passion; a hidden growth score fed by the Learning need passes through eight tiers and the tier decides how many and how good the offered choices are, while a child raised in a growth vat with the Learning need frozen reaches the lowest tier and ends with zero passions, weak skills and three fully random traits ([RimWorld wiki: Children](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Children), [Backstories](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Backstories)). Workers & Resources prices schooling in years and buildings: kindergarten covers ages 0-4 and reaches education level 0.5, school covers 5/6-17 and is required to reach 1.0 which most jobs demand, university runs from 1.0 to a maximum of 3.0, and unfilled kindergarten capacity forces a parent to stay home from work ([Education simulation](https://workers-resources.fandom.com/wiki/Education_simulation)). Songs of Syx makes education a percentage: school raises it to 60% and progresses faster, university closes the rest, 100% requires both to have separately reached 100%, and a fully educated population doubles the settlement's tech points ([Education and Indoctrination](https://songsofsyx.wiki.gg/wiki/Education_and_Indoctrination)). Victoria 3 models literacy as a stock that rises toward a pop's education access and decays when access falls below it ([Vic3 wiki: Pops](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Pops)).

The baseline being replaced is Dwarf Fortress, and it is less purely genetic than it looks. Attributes roll at birth on a species range in bracketed step syntax — dwarven Strength is `[450:950:1150:1250:1350:1550:2250]`, equal chance of each sub-range and of each value inside it, median 1250 ([DF wiki: Attribute](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Attribute)) — but they are **not** frozen: the default cost to improve is 500, the counter increments roughly every 10 ticks of relevant skilled labour, so about 5,000 ticks or a little over four in-game days of continuous work buys +1, capped at the higher of twice the starting value or the starting value plus 1250, and different skills train different attributes. DF also separates **values and beliefs** from **facets**, and says the two can conflict without being incompatible. The genetics page is explicit that colour follows a Mendelian dominant/recessive model inherited from both parents, and equally explicit that whether *attributes* are inherited rather than rolled per individual is unconfirmed — "more research is currently needed to either prove or disprove this" — with one in-game experiment finding they are not inherited in egg-layers ([DF2014: Genetics](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Genetics)). And DF has the one shipped experience-imprints-personality pathway in the sample: a dwarf's eight strongest-emotion events each year are tracked and promoted into permanent long-term memory slots, and those memories permanently shift facets and values over time, desensitisation included ([DF2014: Thoughts and preferences](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Thoughts_and_preferences)). RimWorld is the negative case: traits and thoughts are two systems that never convert into each other, so no amount of lived experience mutates a trait ([Traits](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Traits), [Thoughts](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Thoughts)).

On the eugenics question the survey returns a specific and slightly awkward result. Crusader Kings 3 runs three congenital chains — Attractiveness, Intelligence, Strength — on an active/inactive inheritance model: two active parents give about 80% chance of an active child, one active plus one inactive about 50%, one active parent alone about 25%, two inactive parents 2-10%, and a failed roll usually leaves the trait present inactively for a later generation ([TheGamer guide](https://www.thegamer.com/crusader-kings-3-genetic-traits-guide/)). The CK2 community wiki titles its strategy page ["Breeding: Eugenics For A Better Future"](https://crusaderkings-two.fandom.com/wiki/Breeding:_Eugenics_For_A_Better_Future) without irony, and a critical reading argues the tier naming itself imports eugenic framing and notes CK3's Pure-Blooded trait, earned by generations of deliberate inbreeding, mechanically removes birth-defect risk from a bloodline ([GlitchOut, 2020](https://glitchout.blog/2020/09/28/crusader-kings-3-eugenics-at-play/); fetch blocked, search-snippet sourced). Discomfort with the mechanic is documented outside video games too ([The Fox Experiment on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3258807/theme-is-too-close-to-having-fun-with-eugenics-for), [an itch.io developer thread](https://itch.io/post/9486971)), and *Mewgenics* names the theme in its title ([PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/mewgenics-hands-on-preview/)). What the survey did **not** find is any shipped game or critical source that builds the same arc on selecting for *upbringing* rather than for genome. Every eugenics-flagged mechanic surveyed runs on a persistent biologically inherited layer that the player breeds for. That absence is a synthesis observation from this survey rather than a sourced claim (from memory, unverified).

## 2. Patterns that recur

1. **A world with no edge is a world with no fixed cell count, and every budget written in cells stops being a budget.** Minecraft ticks only the chunks near a player and freezes the rest; Dwarf Fortress simulates one embark at full fidelity and the rest of the world as region tiles; RimWorld runs a local tile map under an abstracted world map. Nobody simulates an unbounded world uniformly, and every one of them draws the same line: a small realised neighbourhood at full detail, everything else as a coarse summary.
2. **Determinism in a lazy world is a property of the generator, not of the run.** A chunk that is a pure function of the seed and its own coordinates can be generated in any order and thrown away freely; a chunk that reads its neighbours cannot, and that is the difference between "regenerate it for free" and "store it forever".
3. **Every distance-derived quantity degrades far from the origin, and the degradation is exponential in the exponent.** Single-precision floats halve their accuracy for every power of two away from zero, so a design that is exact near home is silently approximate a long way out — which is the same class of bug as the Far Lands.
4. **The leaving rule in every foraging model is a comparison against the round trip, not against the patch.** Charnov's marginal value theorem compares the patch's marginal rate against the habitat average *including travel*; central place foraging sets load size by amortising a fixed round-trip cost; giving-up density balances harvest rate against metabolic, predation and opportunity costs. None of them is a fixed threshold on the patch, and a fixed threshold is exactly what our hysteresis bands are.
5. **Which way an agent takes risk flips on whether it can meet its requirement.** Caraco's juncos took the safe perch on a positive budget and the variable one on a negative budget, and individual birds switched when moved between conditions. Every game that puts escalating danger behind a depleting bar is shipping the same shape.
6. **A range extender always costs upkeep that grows faster than the radius it buys.** Frostpunk's Steam Hub Range Upgrade II is +50% range for +100% coal, which its own wiki computes as 225% of the area and "about +10% increase in efficiency". Area is quadratic in radius, so this is geometry rather than balance.
7. **Games substitute a soft failure for death at distance.** Kenshi's KO-instead-of-death plus slaver pickup, Dwarf Fortress's missing status plus corpse hauling plus memorial slab. What both preserve is that the base does not automatically know what happened.
8. **Every shipped childhood system is exposure, sampled at milestones, durable afterwards.** CK3's guardian score resolved around 16, RimWorld's growth moments at 3/7/10/13, Workers & Resources' kindergarten/school/university brackets, Songs of Syx's school-then-university percentages. None of them integrates continuously and none of them lets adulthood keep re-rolling.
9. **The cultural literature's three modes map onto exactly the two mechanisms a colony already has.** Vertical transmission is a household, horizontal transmission is an epidemic and is already the card model, and oblique transmission is a card contact with an adult from outside the household. Adding a channel for schooling would be adding a third name for a thing that already exists.
10. **The clean formulas come with unclean evidence.** The energy budget rule was confirmed in 10 of 35 studies; the Lévy flight hypothesis lost its strongest dataset to a sensor artefact; the Heckman curve is not supported as a monotonic empirical shape and its author now calls it illustrative. Every one of these is a good mechanic and a bad fact, and the design record should say which it is using them as.

## 3. Failure modes to design against

1. **Order-dependent chunk generation destroys replay.** If generating a chunk requires its neighbours, the world depends on the path the colony walked rather than on the seed, and the balance log stops replaying. This is a determinism bug that only shows up on the second run.
2. **A per-agent scan over world content is quadratic in reach.** Our `gather_source` linear-scans every patch for every gathering citizen every tick. Patch count grows with area and area grows with the square of reach, so the cost of the nearest-patch query grows as the fourth power of how far the colony can walk.
3. **Precision failure is invisible until it is catastrophic.** The Far Lands were a noise-coordinate overflow that produced correct-looking terrain right up to the coordinate where it did not. A distance-based heat field has the same shape of failure and no equivalent visual warning.
4. **A reactive threshold with a fixed setpoint is lethal beyond a fixed distance.** Warmth acting at 25 is right thirteen cells out and fatal fifty cells out, because the threshold has no term for the walk home. This is not a tuning problem; no constant works at both distances.
5. **A survival rule applied at one scale contradicts itself at another.** The energy budget rule says a colony short of stores should send haulers further and a citizen short of warmth should come in. Applied naively to the same citizen at the same moment, the two produce a hauler who oscillates on the spot.
6. **An expedition layer whose expected return does not beat staying home is decoration.** RimWorld's caravans are the documented case: players call packing "one of the most painful experiences in the game" and report the cost of a run exceeding the reward. Remove the interface friction and the reward asymmetry is still there.
7. **Rolling a stat at birth makes the colony's whole history irrelevant to who its people are.** That is the current shape and it is what the chapter on cultural stats exists to remove — but the opposite failure is just as real: a stat that is a pure function of colony conditions makes two children of one household identical, and the behaviour-genetics evidence says a large unexplained residual is the empirically honest answer.
8. **Selection on upbringing resolves far faster than selection on genome.** A breeding programme needs generations; a directed-upbringing policy shows its result in one childhood. Any design that gates a policy on "generations of cultural change" has to check that the policy's own payoff is still slow, or the gate protects nothing.
9. **A memorial mechanic that fires automatically removes the loss it is meant to mark.** Dwarf Fortress makes recovering a body cost a second dwarf a trip into whatever killed the first, and raising a slab without the body cost build capacity. Caves of Qud has neither, and its death screen is a scoreboard rather than an event in a world.

## 4. Concrete proposals for our design

Each item: **source mechanic → our mapping → what it conflicts with.**

1. **Make a chunk a pure function of the seed and its own coordinates, and refuse anything that reads a neighbour.** *Source:* Minecraft's cascading worldgen lag, where decoration wrote across chunk borders and forced neighbours to generate, now bounded by discrete per-chunk statuses `empty` → `structure_starts` → ... → `features` → `full` ([Chunk format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk_format)). *Ours:* `noise(seed, salt)` is already a stateless splitmix64 hash of two integers, so `noise(hash(chunk_x, chunk_y), salt)` is evaluable in any order; what has to be forbidden is any placement rule that consults an adjacent chunk. *Conflicts with:* nothing yet, and that is exactly why it should be written down before the first content that wants to straddle a border.
2. **Store only what was changed; regenerate the rest.** *Source:* Minecraft persists generated chunks to 32x32-chunk region files as NBT rather than recomputing them, because edits, entities and tile data would otherwise be lost ([Region file format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Region_file_format)). *Ours:* our mutable world state is small and enumerable — a patch's `amount` against its generated `cap`, and whatever is built — so the sparse store is a map from coordinate to delta and everything else comes back from the seed. This is the cheaper half of Minecraft's design and we can take it because we have no block edits.
3. **Keep the noise position-independent and pick the kind deliberately.** *Source:* gradient noise assigns random gradients at lattice points and interpolates dot products, value noise assigns random values and interpolates them; either evaluates at any coordinate with no prior state, at 2^n corner evaluations in n dimensions ([Perlin 1985](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/325165.325247), [Improving Noise](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/566654.566636)). *Ours:* our `noise` is a value hash and gives no continuity between adjacent cells, which is fine for a per-patch roll and wrong for a terrain field. A biome field needs interpolation between lattice hashes; the cheapest correct thing is value noise on a coarse lattice with a smoothstep, and the honest thing is to say so rather than to call it Perlin.
4. **Adapt Whittaker to one axis, because we have one climate.** *Source:* Whittaker's diagram places biomes in the plane of mean annual temperature against mean annual precipitation ([*Communities and Ecosystems*, 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Whittaker_(ecologist))); Minecraft 1.18 uses six axes (continentalness, erosion, temperature, humidity, weirdness, depth) ([Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)); Dwarf Fortress uses six (elevation, rainfall, temperature, drainage, volcanism, wildness) ([World generation](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation)). *Ours:* our temperature axis is not a field at all — `ambient_at` is global and `heat_at` is a closed form of distance from one point, so the whole colony sits at one climate and the Whittaker temperature axis collapses. What is left is one generated axis, which the second axis of the diagram names for us: how much a cell grows. The adaptation is explicit — we take the *shape* of a biome as a bounded region of a parameter plane, and we supply only one generated parameter, with distance from the hearth as the other.
5. **Let distance from the hearth be the risk axis and the reward axis at once.** *Source:* every world in the sample makes the interesting content sit away from the start, but the sourced version is geometric rather than designed — Terraria's Large world is double Small in each dimension and four times the area ([World size](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/World_size)), and Caves of Qud's parasang is 18,000 cells so the count of anything grows quadratically with reach ([Terminology](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Terminology)). *Ours:* patch richness rises with distance while the warmth cost of reaching it rises linearly, so expected yield per trip has an interior maximum that moves with the season and with what the fire is putting out. *Conflicts with:* nothing, and it is what makes section 14's leash a decision rather than a wall.
6. **Bound every query by a radius, and make the renderer's loop the viewport rather than the world.** *Source:* `HPA*` is about 10x faster than optimised `A*` for paths within 1% of optimal by searching an abstract graph over clusters ([Botea et al. 2004](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228785110_Near_optimal_hierarchical_path-finding_HPA)); flow field tiles compute one field over a **bounded** tile region and share it across agents ([Emerson, Game AI Pro ch. 23](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter23_Crowd_Pathfinding_and_Steering_Using_Flow_Field_Tiles.pdf)); Minecraft ticks only chunks near a player and freezes the rest. *Ours:* `gather_source` gets a bounded search radius and a per-chunk index instead of a linear scan; `regrow_patches` runs over realised chunks only; and `paint_map`, which today allocates a 37x37 grid and repaints 1,369 cells every tick, becomes a viewport that follows the colony. *Conflicts with:* ADR 0005's unbounded hauler radius, which was affordable when the map was 18 cells across.
7. **Keep the world's coordinates integer and its origin at the hearth.** *Source:* accuracy halves for every power of two from the origin, 32-bit floats cannot represent fractions past 8,388,608 units, and the artefact is jitter as vertices snap ([Floating origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_origin)); Minecraft's Far Lands began at about 12,550,821 because a noise value accumulating 171.103 per block overflowed, and were fixed by a modulo 2^24 wraparound ([Far Lands](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Far_Lands)); KSP's Deep Space Kraken is the same failure in physics ([Deep Space Kraken](https://kerbalspaceprogram.fandom.com/wiki/Deep_Space_Kraken)). *Ours:* positions stay `IVec2`, `CENTER` moves from (18, 18) to the origin, and the only f32 in the spatial path is `heat_at`'s distance — which is exact while coordinates stay under 2^24, a bound the fifty-cell leash of section 14 puts far out of reach. Recording it is cheap insurance against a later mechanic that moves the hearth. *Conflicts with:* nothing today; `CENTER` is referenced 47 times, so the change is mechanical.

8. **Turn for home on a comparison against the walk, not against a constant.** *Source:* Charnov's marginal value theorem, "leave the patch when the marginal capture rate in the patch drops to the average capture rate for the habitat", with travel counted as dead time in the cycle ([Charnov 1976](https://digitalrepository.unm.edu/biol_fsp/9/)). *Ours:* warmth's acting threshold becomes `cost_of_return + caution_margin` rather than the constant 25, where the cost of return is the Chebyshev distance to the nearest heat times the per-tick warmth loss. The currency is substituted: the theorem's is energy, ours is body heat. *Conflicts with:* ADR 0003, which makes threshold behaviour a pair of constants.
9. **Let a distant patch be stripped further than a near one.** *Source:* the same theorem's counterintuitive result, that longer travel means longer patch residence, tested on great tits by making the dish lids harder to open ([Cowie 1977](https://www.nature.com/articles/268137a0)). *Ours:* the give-up point on a patch scales with how far it is, so a treeline forty cells out is worked down further than one at thirteen. *Conflicts with:* nothing — `gather_source` currently has no give-up rule at all, only "nearest patch with anything in it".
10. **Scale the load with the distance.** *Source:* Orians and Pearson's central place foraging, where a fixed round-trip cost amortised over the load predicts bigger loads from farther patches, seen in honeybee nectar loads and beaver tree diameters ([central place foraging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_place_foraging)). *Ours:* `food_yield` already makes a haul worth more without changing the walk; add a distance term so a long trip brings back more. *Conflicts with:* nothing, but the evidence is a tendency and at least one study found no significant load-by-distance interaction, so treat it as a knob.
11. **Apply the energy budget rule at two scales and let them disagree.** *Source:* Caraco's juncos took the constant perch on a positive budget and the variable one on a negative budget, and individuals switched when moved between conditions ([Caraco et al. 1980](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347280801424), [Stephens 1981](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232573336_The_logic_of_risk-sensitive_foraging_preferences)). *Ours:* the colony's budget is `stock_share` against the per-head target and pushes haulers further when negative; the citizen's budget is their own warmth and pulls them in when low. The citizen who dies is the one a hungry colony sent on a day their own reserve was thin. *Conflicts with:* nothing, but the rule is empirically weak — only 10 of 35 studies confirmed the switch ([Houston 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/brv.13031)) — so it is adopted as a stylised mechanic.
12. **Price a waystation the way Frostpunk prices a Steam Hub, and correct the geometry.** *Source:* a hub is 20 Steel and 3 coal an hour at range 1, 6 at range 2; Range Upgrade II is +50% range for +100% coal, which the wiki computes as 225% of the heated area and concedes is "about +10% increase in efficiency" ([Steam Hub](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Steam_Hub), [Range Upgrade II](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Steam_Hub_Range_Upgrade_II_(Technology))). *Ours:* our heat is a linear falloff rather than a radius band, so a source of output `h` reaches `h / HEAT_FALLOFF` cells — radius linear in output, area quadratic. A boiler's `UPGRADE_HEAT` of 18 buys six cells of ring for everyone; a brazier of the same output at a treeline forty cells out buys forty cells for whoever works there and nothing at the centre. *Conflicts with:* the efficiency tier of the ballot, which currently answers every detour with a boiler.
13. **Make a death out of sight a disappearance, and make closing it cost something.** *Source:* Dwarf Fortress gives a unit a missing status the instant it leaves the map; an unburied corpse gives unhappy thoughts to family and can raise a ghost; a memorial slab closes it without the body and reports the death when read; corpse hauling is a real labour the wiki warns against giving to depression-prone dwarves ([Corpse](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Corpse), [Slab](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Slab), [Hauling](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Hauling)). Caves of Qud is the negative case with no such mechanic at all ([Death](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Death)). *Ours:* the chronicle records that a citizen did not come back, not that they died; recovering the body costs a second citizen the same trip, and a slab costs build capacity instead. *Conflicts with:* nothing, and it gives section 10's chronicle an event type it cannot otherwise generate.
14. **Imprint caution through the card channel and add nothing.** *Source:* Dwarf Fortress promotes a dwarf's eight strongest-emotion events each year into permanent memory that shifts facets and values ([Thoughts and preferences](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Thoughts_and_preferences)); RimWorld is the negative case where thoughts and traits never convert ([Traits](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Traits)). *Ours:* an expedition death is a colony event, colony events already birth cards, and the card raises the survivors' caution margin. *Conflicts with:* nothing — it is section 8's existing card-birth rule reading a new event.
15. **Form stats through the two channels that exist, and name the third rather than building it.** *Source:* Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman's vertical, oblique and horizontal transmission, with horizontal modelled explicitly as an epidemic ([PMC retrospective](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11621465/), [SIS formalisation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0040580913000907)). *Ours:* household is vertical and ADR 0009 already runs it, cards are horizontal and ADR 0006 already runs them, and the oblique channel is a card contact with an adult outside the child's household — a school is a place where those contacts happen, not a new mechanism. *Conflicts with:* nothing, and it is what keeps the count of transmission systems at two.
16. **Make the childhood accumulator multiplicative and resolve it at milestones.** *Source:* Cunha and Heckman's self-productivity and dynamic complementarity — skills persist and raise the productivity of later investment, and investment is worth more against a higher existing stock ([NBER WP 12840](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12840/w12840.pdf)); every shipped game samples at discrete ages rather than continuously (CK3 around 16, RimWorld at 3/7/10/13). *Ours:* `met_shares` is already computed every tick and already feeds `maturation_rate`, so the accumulator costs one field per child and no new scan, and it resolves at named ages rather than per tick. *Conflicts with:* section 1's budget only if it resolves per tick, which is why it does not.
17. **Weight the window by age, and let hardiness and strength catch up where wits cannot.** *Source:* Dutch Hunger Winter women exposed before age 1 reached 4.45 cm less adult height and before age 2 4.08 cm less ([*Economics & Human Biology*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1570677X1630140X)); the stunting window runs nine months pre-birth to twenty-four months post ([AJCN](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23553163/)); height catch-up frequently occurs between 24 months and adulthood while cognitive effects persist ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7754567/), [A4NH](https://a4nh.cgiar.org/2020/09/10/catch-up-growth-cannot-undo-damage-from-undernutrition/)). *Ours:* the accumulator is age-weighted heaviest at the start, hardiness and strength keep an adolescent catch-up term, wits do not. *Conflicts with:* nothing, and it is what makes a colony's recovery from a bad winter legible in the children who lived through it.
18. **Keep `lifespan_of(seed)` as the residual and cite why.** *Source:* Plomin and Daniels found most environmental variance in personality is nonshared, and Plomin's 2024 update reports measured predictors explain 1-2% of variance, so the channel behaves as individual stochasticity ([PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11472802/)); Turkheimer's third law says a substantial share of variance is explained by neither genes nor shared family environment ([Turkheimer 2000](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8721.00084)); the Wilson effect puts shared-environment variance in IQ near zero from age 12 ([Bouchard 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23919982/)). *Ours:* the existing roll stops deciding a citizen and becomes the unexplained term on top of what the colony raised, which is the legibility requirement — two children of one house must not be clones — with evidence instead of a hand-wave. *Conflicts with:* ADR 0009, which currently derives frailty from that roll alone.
19. **Adopt the mechanism of the Heckman curve and not its shape.** *Source:* Rea and Burton find the broader evidence does not support a monotonically declining return with age ([*Journal of Economic Surveys* 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joes.12353?af=R)) and Heckman has since called the curve illustrative rather than estimated ([Gelman](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2019/04/06/heckman-curve-update-data-dont-seem-support-claim-human-capital-investments-effective-targeted-younger-ages/)). *Ours:* self-productivity and dynamic complementarity are used; the claim that early investment always returns more is not. *Conflicts with:* nothing, but it is the reason the age weighting in proposal 15 comes from the stunting literature rather than from economics.
20. **Give hardiness an acclimatisation term rather than locking it at fifteen.** *Source:* the Korean and Japanese diving women ran basal metabolic rate about 5% above standard in summer against 35% in winter, and the seasonal swing disappeared once wetsuits cut the exposure after 1977 ([*Journal of Applied Physiology* 1963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31090384/), [*Journal of Physiological Anthropology* 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40101-017-0146-6)). *Ours:* on top of whatever childhood set, hardiness carries a slow term fed by hours actually spent below freezing and decaying when they stop. *Conflicts with:* nothing, and it is the only honest way to have cold-hardened citizens given what the literature actually supports.
21. **Check the eugenics arc against its new clock.** *Source:* every eugenics-flagged mechanic surveyed runs on an inherited genome the player breeds for — CK3's three congenital chains with active/inactive inheritance at 80/50/25/2-10% ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/crusader-kings-3-genetic-traits-guide/)), CK2's community wiki page titled "Breeding: Eugenics For A Better Future" ([CK2 Fandom](https://crusaderkings-two.fandom.com/wiki/Breeding:_Eugenics_For_A_Better_Future)) — and the survey found none built on selecting for upbringing instead. *Ours:* ADR 0006's gating machinery is untouched, but its premise that the payoff is generations away is not: a directed-upbringing policy pays out in one childhood, fifteen calendar years. *Conflicts with:* ADR 0006, directly, on pacing rather than on mechanism.

## 5. What I could not source

- **RimWorld's total world-tile count** depends on the planet-coverage setting and no single figure could be pinned down; the 250x250 default local map and the hex world map above it are sourced, the tile total is not.
- **Frostpunk expedition travel times and per-trip supply consumption** are not tabulated anywhere on the wiki, and the per-outpost daily yields (800 coal a day from a coal mine, 1 steam core a day from Tesla City) come from a third-party guide rather than the wiki, so they are indicative rather than authoritative.
- **Frostpunk heat zones from different sources not stacking** is community knowledge and is not stated in the current wiki text; the zone naming that circulates ("not heated / warm / medium warmth / high warmth") does not appear on the wiki at all, which uses the Comfortable-to-Freezing scale instead.
- **Kenshi's exact cold and heat damage per tick** could not be retrieved — every fetch of that page was blocked — and neither could its per-region danger scaling or the leg-health-to-speed curve below the 55.6% running threshold.
- **The Long Dark's interior shelter temperatures** are not documented with numbers on its wiki.
- **Brown's original giving-up-density notation** is paywalled everywhere; `H = C + P + MOC` is a convergent paraphrase across independent secondary sources, not a verbatim quote from the 1988 paper.
- **Binford's exact wording** for the forager/collector distinction is paraphrased from secondary sources; the primary text was not retrievable.
- **Cold-water survival-time charts by temperature band** are widely mirrored but could not be traced to a primary NOAA or USCG document, and cold-water-safety researchers explicitly call this class of chart and the related "1-10-1 rule" misleading ([coldwatersafety.org](https://www.coldwatersafety.org/1-10-1-myth)), so no human cold-exposure survival curve is cited in this report at all.
- **Dwarf Fortress's one-week delay** between a unit going missing and the formal announcement comes from a search summary rather than a direct read of the unit-list page, and is lower confidence than the corpse and slab findings around it.
- **CK3's education-tier score thresholds** could not be pinned to one number: two credible sources give 0-7/8-12/13-17/18+ and 11-15/15+ respectively, which is almost certainly a patch-version difference rather than an error in either.
- **Whether Dwarf Fortress inherits attributes from parents** is explicitly unconfirmed on its own genetics page — "more research is currently needed to either prove or disprove this" — with one in-game experiment finding they are not inherited in egg-layers. Colour inheritance is confirmed Mendelian; attributes are not.
- **A game or a critical source that builds a eugenics arc on selecting for upbringing rather than for genome** was not found. Every eugenics-flagged mechanic surveyed runs on an inherited genome. That absence is a synthesis observation from this survey rather than a sourced claim (from memory, unverified).
- **RimWorld backstory skill offsets** by name (the specific "+N to skill X" per backstory) could not be extracted; the mechanic is confirmed, the numbers are not.
- **Songs of Syx per-species learning rates** are referenced by its wiki as existing but the rate table was not accessible.
