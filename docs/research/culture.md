# Culture, ideology, legitimacy and contested authority in games — research notes

Scope: how shipped games encode (a) multi-axis culture and its drift, (b) explicit acceptability/Overton gating, (c) legitimacy-type resources and what they gate, (d) organised pushback, (e) memes/traditions with carriers and decay, (f) the pricing of harsh policy. Every mechanic claim carries a link. Sentences marked "(from memory, unverified)" have no source and should be treated as hypotheses.

## 1. Per-game findings

### Democracy 4 (Positech / Cliff Harris) — the only shipped explicit Overton window

Democracy 3 already had the voter-group model, political capital and cynicism; the Overton window was introduced during Democracy 4 development and shipped as its own dev blog entry, [Developer Blog #14: The Overton Window](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2248115628). Treat everything below as Democracy 4 unless stated.

**(a) Axes and drift.** Voters sit on a two-axis compass: socialism–capitalism and liberalism–conservatism. Each voter has an *innate* position; the displayed position is a blend with the national consensus. Cliffski gives the formula verbatim: `Final Socialism = (CurrentGlobalSocialism * Impressionability) + (InnateSocialism * (1-Impressionability))`, where Impressionability is drawn from a normal distribution **clamped to 15%–85%** so the population can never become uniform, and a `Polarization` situation pushes innate positions outward ([Improvements to the political compass / voters opinions](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/03/03/improvements-to-the-political-compass-voters-opinions/)).

**(b) The window itself.** The Overton window is implemented as a "flexible overton splat" — a region of the compass containing the majority of voters; policies are expected to stay inside it, and voters are "less confined to the Overton window, and yet are still influenced by it" ([same post](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/03/03/improvements-to-the-political-compass-voters-opinions/)). Critically, the window **prices** rather than **forbids**: the player "can implement anything with enough political capital" ([Liberalism/Socialism tweaks](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/06/06/liberalism-socialism-tweaks/)). AI parties also drift toward a winning player's position, which is how the window moves at the party level ([D4 category archive](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/category/democracy-4/page/14/)).

**(c) Legitimacy analogue = Political Capital.** Every introduce / cancel / raise / lower action on a policy has its own political-capital price, declared per policy in the moddable CSVs ([D4 policy modding reference](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_policies.html)). Capital is generated per turn by ministers, scaled by their loyalty; a real (or manufactured) crisis grants **emergency powers that raise the cap to ~50 versus a typical high-20s** ([D4 basic gameplay guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/8590-democracy-4.html)).

**(f) Two separate timescales per policy.** The designer explicitly split *immediate anger* from *slow normalisation*: legalising cannabis angers conservatives on the turn it passes, but its long-run effect is "increased liberalism, and people get used to the idea" ([Liberalism/Socialism tweaks](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/06/06/liberalism-socialism-tweaks/)). Gradualism is also priced: slider changes have variable cost, "smaller changes are easier" than radical jumps ([D4 category archive](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/category/democracy-4/page/14/)).

**(d) Pushback.** Terrorism is a hidden national value; angry voters first join pressure groups, then "particularly angry members of those groups may, over time, become 'radicalized' enough to join a dangerous terrorist organization", producing attacks and assassination attempts ([D4 basic gameplay guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/8590-democracy-4.html), [Terrorism — Democracy Wiki](https://democracygame.fandom.com/wiki/Terrorism)). **Cynicism** punishes inconsistency specifically: it rises from flip-flopping (enact then cancel), from bribing voters just before an election, and from hurting them just after one, and it only decays by being forgotten ([Cynicism — Democracy Wiki](https://democracygame.fandom.com/wiki/Cynicism), summarised in the [gameplay guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/8590-democracy-4.html)).

**Failure.** Protest/tactical voting was absent at launch and Cliffski was still designing it in Oct 2021, sketching ~10% of the electorate as protest-prone and triggering when polling exceeds ~70% ([Protest votes in Democracy 4](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/10/25/protest-votes-in-democracy-4-final-piece-of-the-puzzle/)). The compass also initially "was not accurately reflecting my policy choices" and needed a pass over which policies write to the global values ([D4 category archive](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/category/democracy-4/page/14/)) — i.e. the axis→policy mapping is the part that breaks.

### Frostpunk 1 (11 bit studios) — irreversibility as the whole design

**(a/e) No axes.** Culture is a single **tree**, the Book of Laws, split into Adaptation (always open) and Purpose, and Purpose forks into **mutually exclusive** Order and Faith branches ([Book of Laws — official wiki](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Book_of_Laws), [game-vault](https://frostpunk.game-vault.net/wiki/Book_of_laws)). There are no carriers; the colony is one homogeneous body.

**(b/f) Gating is by irreversibility and rate limit, not by acceptability.** Laws **cannot be repealed** — a "recall law" option existed in an early trailer and was cut — and there is an **18-hour cooldown** between signings ([Steam: Can you repeal laws?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1474222595302046365/), [Steam: How do you repeal laws?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/3150808675339629743/)). The Purpose tree is deliberately ordered so each law is *slightly* more extreme than the one before, so that "the sum creates something more terrifying" — the designers name this **creeping normality / boiling frog**, modelled on how total institutions are born ([design analysis with dev sourcing](https://www.gabrielchauri.com/frostpunk-decisions/), [11 bit on the Book of Laws](https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/)). Signing any of the last three laws of either Purpose path changes the epilogue to "We have crossed the line… But was it worth it?" ([Book of Laws — official wiki](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Book_of_Laws)).

**(c) Hope and Discontent, 0–100 each.** Discontent at ~100 or Hope at 0 does **not** end the game immediately: the player gets an ultimatum to move the offending bar by **15% within 2 days**; failure means mutiny, banishment, and in extreme cases execution ([gamepressure: what happens at critical values](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c), [Discontent — wiki](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Discontent)). Per-law and per-event deltas are deliberately hidden from the UI, scale with difficulty, and are only recoverable from game files; players established that Hope loss from deaths is temporary (~24h) and that Ceremonial Funerals **halve** it ([Steam: what are the specific Discontent and Hope values?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)).

**Failure.** The dominant critique is that the authoritarian ramp is a *mechanically dominant strategy*: keep passing control laws up to public executions and "hope will never be a problem again", at which point the attached hope/discontent numbers stop mattering. The game's only answer is a judgemental epilogue — a narrative cost, not a systemic one ([critical roundup](https://www.newgamenetwork.com/article/1892/frostpunk-review/); academic treatment in [Imbierowicz, DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2025/2024/2021)). Second critique: two aggregate bars reduce citizens to statistics, which is precisely the abstraction the game wanted to interrogate.

### Frostpunk 2 (11 bit studios) — three axes, a real parliament, and a trust resource

**(a) Zeitgeist = 3 axes, 6 poles.** Survival: Adaptation↔Progress; Economy: Equality↔Merit; Society: Tradition↔Reason. Which axis a choice moves depends on where it comes from: Heating/Resources/Frostland ideas move Progress–Adaptation, City ideas move Merit–Equality, Society ideas and Laws move Tradition–Reason ([Zeitgeist — official wiki](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Zeitgeist), [game-vault Zeitgeist](https://frostpunk-2.game-vault.net/wiki/Zeitgeist)). **Communities hold a position on one axis only; Factions commit on all three and "usually cannot be convinced by negotiations"; communities are larger and harder to radicalise** ([gamepressure: Factions](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/factions/zc113e2)).

**(b/c) Acceptability is a literal vote.** The Council seats **100 delegates** apportioned by population share; a law needs **51**, and laws in the *Rule* category — those that increase the Steward's personal power — need a **supermajority of 67** ([Twinfinite council guide](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-2-council-voting-explained-how-to-pass-every-vote-negotiate-more/), [gamepressure: Council](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/council/zf115fb)). Delegates are For / Against / **Hesitant**, the last being a coin flip. There is a **10-week cooldown between votes** and a failed bill cannot return until the next session. Votes are bought with **Promises** — the game offers 3 randomly-drawn demands and the player picks 1; breaking it "worsens relations significantly" — or with **Pressure**, which always adds votes but damages relations with *every* faction ([gamepressure: Council](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/council/zf115fb), [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-2-council-voting-explained-how-to-pass-every-vote-negotiate-more/)).

**(c) Trust is the legitimacy resource.** 0–100, banded Revered / Respected / Accepted / Tolerated / Despised; falling to Despised triggers an ultimatum to climb **from 0% back to 25%**, and failing it is game over. Trust is "harder to earn than to lose" and is drained by non-natural deaths, starvation, disease and crime ([pcgamesn: how to increase trust](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/trust), [game-vault Trust](https://frostpunk-2.game-vault.net/wiki/Trust)). The 2026 *Breach of Trust* DLC makes it the terminal resource outright: a running Vote of Trust gauge, and dropping below threshold removes the player with "no emergency session, no second chance" ([techtimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318912/20260623/frostpunk-2-breach-trust-launches-volcanoes-new-city-vote-that-can-end-your-rule.htm)).

**(d/f) Fervour, Tension, and the price of suppression.** Fervour is per-faction, shown as red diamonds, and represents members "ready to remove you from power by violence"; it rises when the city's direction contradicts the faction's cornerstones, when promises are broken, and — after patch 1.1.0 — **enacting a Cornerstone adds exactly +2 fervour to the faction aligned with the opposed affinity** ([patch 1.1.0 notes](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/frostpunk-2-patch-1-1-0-release-notes), [thegamer: reduce fervor](https://www.thegamer.com/frostpunk-2-how-to-deradicalize-factions/)). Fervour feeds **Tension**, a city-wide orb also fed by cold, hunger, disease, squalor and crime; max Tension is game over ([game8: how to decrease tension](https://game8.co/games/Frostpunk-2/archives/474374)). Two removal routes, and the contrast is the interesting part: **Deradicalize** peels members back into an allied *community* (needs good relations with a community sharing the faction's cornerstone — cheap, consensual), versus **Round Up Faction Members** from a Prison district, which arrests **up to 500** members over several weeks, cutting Fervour but causing "a large increase in your city's Tension" ([sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/how-deradicalize-factions-frostpunk-2), [gamerant](https://gamerant.com/frostpunk-2-deradicalize-factions-guide-tips/)). Neglected factions also **splinter**: ignoring the traditionalist Frostlanders spawns the more extreme Pilgrims ([actpotential essay](https://actpotential.substack.com/p/frostpunk-2-is-a-video-game-about)).

**Designer intent.** Game Director Jakub Stokalski: "You don't set out to do evil things because you want to be evil, you set out to do these things because you are driven by a certain set of values" — the council/zeitgeist frame exists so that cruelty is anchored to a value position rather than to player whim; they explicitly did not want a "jackass simulator" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/frostpunk-2-s-developers-didn-t-want-it-to-be-a-jackass-simulator-)).

**Failure — the most important item in this report.** The council is described in reviews as "the defining, most brilliant, and often most frustrating element": *the more you appease factions and democratise, the more paralysed the government becomes*, with laws weaponised by opponents ([pcgamesn review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/review)). Players report the pushback reads as **scripted rather than earned** — "in the final act, one of them will act up and drag down their opposite whether you have kept them happy or not", and "most likely though they will radicalize and riot eventually" ([Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1601580/discussions/0/583876502953702073)). Players also report the population feels less real than in FP1 despite being politically richer. Two lessons: a faction system that always ends in revolt teaches players their input does not matter, and a negotiation layer with a 10-week cadence becomes an errand queue.

### Victoria 3 — the closest shipped thing to an explicit acceptability gate

**(b/c) Laws are a repeated roll whose odds are literally the sum of political clout.** From the wiki, verbatim structure: `Base success chance = Σ(Clout of endorsing interest groups in government) + Σ(Support of endorsing non-passive political movements) + Ruler's endorsement` (the ruler contributes **±5% per ideological step** from the current law), and `Base stall chance = 0.5×Σ(clout of neutral/happier opposing IGs) + Σ(clout of unhappy opposing) + 1.5×Σ(clout of angry opposing) + Σ(support of opposing movements) + Ruler's opposition`, with `Advance chance = 2 × Base success − Base stall`. Checkpoints fire every **100 days** by default — **150** for Distribution of Power, **200** for Governance Principles, Economic System and Slavery — and **3 accumulated setbacks fail the law, which then cannot be re-proposed for 2 years** ([Vic3 wiki: Laws](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Laws)).

**(c) Legitimacy, 0–100, five bands, and a real hard gate.** Illegitimate <25, Unacceptable 25–49, Contested 50–74, Legitimate 75–89, Righteous 90+. It is computed from the total clout of the interest groups in government plus, where elections exist, their vote share; each ideological disagreement between government IGs costs a base **−5**, and each IG over the government size allowance costs **−20**. Effects run from Illegitimate (**+3.0% radical pop generation**) to Righteous (**+0.05% radicals, −25% opposition approval**) ([Vic3 wiki: Government](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Government)). **The gate: an illegitimate government (<25) cannot pass laws at all unless a political movement endorsing that law has at least 25 activism** ([Vic3 wiki: Laws](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Laws)). This is the one shipped mechanic in the sample that *forbids* rather than prices — and note it forbids based on the ruler's standing, with an escape hatch through organised popular support.

**(d) Movements: formation is self-limiting, activism is recency-weighted.** A movement needs **≥2.5 support** to form and disbands below **1**, and cannot disband until **12 months** have passed; formation chance rises with support but is **divided by (1 + 2 × number of active movements)**, so the political field cannot fragment indefinitely. Support = 0.34% per percentage of population supporting + 0.33% per percentage of military + 0.33% per point of raw pop political power. **Activism moves 1% per week toward a target** set by law stances — **±5 per enacted law but ±25 per *recently* enacted law** — and by pop composition: **radical supporting pops +100, loyalist supporting pops −75**, each scaled by their share of supporters ([Vic3 wiki: Political movements](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Political_movements)).

**Failures.** The whole movement layer is itself a **rework**: 1.8's Dev Diary #128 "Political Movement Rework" replaced the previous system and "completely revamped the underlying code around how pops are attracted to interest groups", with agitators becoming fixated on specific ideas and unavailable when no movement they care about can exist ([DD #128](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-128-political-movement-rework.1704134/), [DD #130 – Radicalism and Civil Wars](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-130-political-movement-radicalism-and-civil-wars.1706965/)). Two years after launch Paradox rebuilt the pop→faction attraction model — the same component Stellaris rebuilt in 4.3. That is the part of this design space that is hardest to get right, in two separate studios' shipped games.

### Crusader Kings 3 — traditions as carriers, acceptance with anti-ratchet decay, legitimacy as a level

**(e) Traditions are the carriers.** A culture holds **5 tradition slots in the Tribal era, +1 per era reached**, across 5 categories (Realm, Warfare, Social, Ritual, Regional). Adding one costs **2000 Prestige base, +2000 if the culture lacks a preferred ethos, +3000 if an optional requirement is unmet**; *replacing* an existing tradition costs **+50% and takes +25% longer**, and establishment time **caps at 30 years** scaling with county count ([CK3 wiki: Culture](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Culture), [Traditions](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Traditions)). Cultures also **Diverge** (500 Prestige) or **Hybridize** (1000 Prestige, requires a different Heritage pillar) — and hybrids get their traditions immediately, no delay ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/crusader-kings-3-ck3-how-diverge-hybridize-cultures/)).

**(a) Acceptance, 0–100, with decay that prevents a ratchet.** Static baseline is built from shared heritage (+20), language (+10), ethos+region (+10), faith (+10) etc. Yearly change adds **+0.05/year per bordering county capped at +1.0**, up to +0.5 from a mixed realm, war penalties of **−1/−2/−3/−4** by rank, and crucially **−0.01/year for every point above the static baseline** — an equilibrium pull that stops unrelated cultures from drifting to 100 ([Culture](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Culture), yearly values via [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/crusader-kings-3-how-to-increase-raise-cultural-acceptance/)). Acceptance **40** gates hybridization (**80** if either side has *Staunch Traditionalists*).

**(c) Fervor is a self-correcting window.** 0–100, **+3.5/year**, growing more slowly for faiths in **more than 20 counties**; new faiths start at 100, historical faiths at 50. **Below 40% fervor** an Organized faith risks a heresy outbreak converting **10–25 counties and at least one Duke** — and every ruler who defects gives **+15% fervor back to the religion they left** ([CK3 wiki: Faith](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Faith)). Losing your extremes literally re-concentrates the core. The wiki gives **no numeric** fervor→cost or fervor→conversion-speed coefficient (unverified in guides).

**(c) Legitimacy (Roads to Power, 1.13).** Scale 0–2800 across 8 levels; thresholds are **base 30/70/120/200/300 multiplied by title rank** (a King needs 90/210/360/600/900) and rise each era. Effects at Kingdom rank run from Illegitimate (**+75% Short Reign, +100% claim CB cost, −50 alliance/marriage acceptance, −20 popular opinion, −0.2 renown, increased claimant factions**) to Ordained (**−50% Short Reign, −75% claim cost, +50 acceptance, +10 popular opinion, +0.25 renown, −20% counties needed to create a title**) ([CK3 wiki: Attributes](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Attributes)). There is **no** direct levy/tax/MaA modifier — the pressure is entirely via factions and vassal *expectation* (dukes expect Rightful, powerful vassals always demand one level higher). **Legitimacy does not decay naturally.** Sourced gains: Hold Court **+50**, House Feast/Grand Wedding **+10**, creating a legend about someone **+20** (**+50** for a virtuous subject).

**Failures.** 1.5 slowed culture conversion and added **three game rules** to throttle hybrid/divergent formation because hybrids "can easily convert their parent cultures for a long while after forming" ([Patch 1.5](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Patch_1.5)); 1.13 "broadly reduced cultural acceptance gains from events" and rebalanced 40+ traditions, explicitly "removed some inconsequential modifiers" ([Patch 1.13](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Patch_1.13)) — Paradox conceding a chunk of the tradition list was filler. **Legitimacy is the most disliked of the three**: complaints are that activity-spam is the only reliable source, that losses are asymmetric (a lost minor battle costs ~50, a won war gives little), and the most popular Legitimacy Workshop mod **removes the feature entirely** ([Paradox forum: "Legitimacy is badly implemented"](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/legitimacy-is-badly-implemented-how-do-we-fix-it.1673331/)).

### Stellaris — attraction as a multiplicative field, factions with no teeth

**(a) Ethics.** 4 opposed axes (Authoritarian–Egalitarian, Spiritualist–Materialist, Militarist–Pacifist, Xenophile–Xenophobe), **3 points to spend: moderate = 1, fanatic = 2, gestalt = 3** ([Stellaris wiki: Ethics](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Ethics)).

**(a) Drift formula.** Per-pop attraction is a product of multipliers (percentage terms additive among themselves, multiplicative with the rest). Universal: **empire holds the ethic as Fanatic ×3, as Moderate ×2, a faction for it exists ×2, planetary governor has it ×1.5, sector governor ×1.25, +10% per councillor**. Situational examples: Militarist ×3.00 if a planet is occupied, ×2.00 at war; Pacifist ×1.33 per 20-year peace band (stacking at 20/40/60/80/100 years) while Militarist drops to ×0.75; Egalitarian ×1.50 for an enslaved pop; Authoritarian ×1.25 if the empire has no elections. **Governing Ethics Attraction** — the pull toward the state's official ethics — is driven by happiness: *"each percentage point of happiness above or below 50 adds the same amount"*, so a pop at 70% happiness contributes +20%. Edicts stack on top (Hearts and Minds **+100%**, One Vision **+50%**, Conformists trait **+30%**, catastrophic consumer-goods deficit **−100%**). **Hive-minded pops multiply by ×0 — they never drift.**

**(d) Factions.** Form **10 years** in (1 year with Broken Shackles, 3 months with Parliamentary System), needing **≥500 pops** with citizenship and **≥3% attraction**, with a **180-day cooldown** between formations. Approval 0–100, base 50, moved by *issues* worth +10/+5/−30 etc.; opposing-ethic factions hold opposing issues so universal approval is impossible by construction. Approval bands set member happiness: **0–19% → −40%, 20–39% → −10%, 40–59% → 0, 60–79% → +5%, 80–100% → +10%**. **Embrace** costs **5000 Unity**, needs **≥20% support**, has a **10-year lockout**, and gives the embraced faction **+10% approval while every other faction takes −35% for 10 years**. **Suppress = −75% ethic attraction and −50% approval** (and is free since 3.3). Rebellion is **not** wired to factions: unhappiness → planet Stability, and *"colonies with less than **25 Stability** for a year or more can trigger a Planetary Revolt situation"* ([Factions](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Factions), [Stability](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Unrest), [Patch 3.3](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Patch_3.3)).

**Failures.** Factions have **no downside case** — an angry faction only costs you forgone Unity, never negative Unity, so the entire layer is ignorable; a bug report arguing otherwise was closed **As Designed** ([forum thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/issues-regarding-unity-from-faction-approval.1506907/), [closed report](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/stellaris-faction-unity-gain-is-messed-up-bad-v3-5-1-73bb.1543571/)). Ethics-as-per-pop-state was also the **performance** bottleneck: 4.0 keyed Pop Groups on species+strata+ethics+faction and the group count exploded, so **4.3 removed the ethics split entirely** — a pop group now holds *multiple ethics as percentages* — with the stated goal of making conversion *"more reliable and more frequent"* ([Dev Diary #370](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-370-4-0-changes-part-4.1728047/), [Dev Diary #411](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-411-our-next-4-3-experiment.1904297/)). That is Paradox stating on the record that per-carrier ideological drift was unreliable for years.

**(e) Legends — a shipped memetic-spread system worth stealing.** *Legends of the Dead* (1.12) adds narratives that **spread barony by barony**: quality upgrades from Famed → **Illustrious at 100 baronies** → **Mythical at 300 baronies**. Creating one costs **200 Gold (Count/Duke) / 300 (King) / 400 (Emperor)** plus monthly upkeep, Legitimizing legends also cost **75 Prestige**, and a legend needs **at least 5 years** before it can be completed or abandoned. Other rulers can become **Promoters**, getting reduced bonuses for **half the maintenance**. Spread legends give county modifiers (MaA damage, control growth, development) — and **completing a legend ends the owner/promoter bonuses** ([CK3 wiki: Legends](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Legends)).

### RimWorld: Ideology — the shipped model closest to our tradition cards

**(e) Memes are the cards.** An ideoligion holds **up to 4 memes**, each with an *impact* tier that determines how many and how extreme its attached precepts are. A **Fluid** ideoligion starts with only **1 meme, and it cannot be a high-impact meme**; new memes are bought with **development points** earned from rituals, and reform costs escalate — **10 points for the first reform, 12 for the next**, and so on ([RimWorld wiki: Ideoligion](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideoligion), [Ideology Min-Maxing](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Ideology_Min-Maxing)). Structure (Archist, etc.) is flavour with no mechanical effect — a designer decision worth noting: they cut the axis that did not change decisions.

**(a/e) Certainty is the carrier's grip on the card.** Per-pawn, 0–100%; at 0 the pawn switches ideoligion. The conversion formula is multiplicative: `Certainty loss = 6% × Conversion Power of converter × (×2 if converter is Moral Guide) × Global Certainty Loss Factor of convertee × Relic Conversion Power Factor × trait/meme agreeableness × storyteller low-pop boost` ([Certainty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty), [Global Certainty Loss Factor](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Global_Certainty_Loss_Factor)). The Moral Guide's *Convert* ability is the same interaction on demand with a **3-day cooldown**.

**(f) The conversion ritual is the harsh-vs-gentle tradeoff in miniature.** Outcome distribution at base is Terrible 0 / Ineffective **5%** / Effective **60%** / Masterful **20%** (plus modifiers: **+40%** for 10 participants, **+20%** for room impressiveness 120, **+30%** for a Moral Guide at 150% Social Impact, **+10%** if the convertee's expectations are Very Low but **−30%** if Sky High). Certainty is then offset by **terrible +20%, ineffective +12%, effective −30%, masterful −100%** — a botched conversion **hardens** the target ([RimWorld wiki: Rituals](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Rituals)). A leader's speech shifts listeners' opinion of them by **−30 / −15 / +20 / +40** across the four quality tiers — legitimacy generated by performance, per listener.

**(f) Suppression is a leaky bucket, which is the key anti-tyranny term.** RimWorld slavery: slave suppression **decays by up to 20% per day**, base slave rebellion interval is **45 days** modified by suppression, and terror apparel adds **+15% Slave Suppression Offset** per collar and per body strap (**+30%** together). Suppression is raised by interacting with, melee-attacking, or **executing other slaves or prisoners** ([Slavery](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Slavery), [Slave Suppression Offset](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Slave_Suppression_Offset)). Because it decays, coercion is an *ongoing* upkeep cost, never a one-time purchase — exactly the property Frostpunk 1's authoritarian ramp lacks.

### Dwarf Fortress — values and facets as two orthogonal per-citizen vectors

**(a)** Each dwarf carries **personality values** on a **−50 … +50** scale reported in **seven bands**, with the **−10 … +10** middle band producing no report at all — i.e. weak convictions are deliberately invisible. Separately, each carries **personality facets** on a **0–100** scale, also in seven bands, with **40–60** silent. The wiki states the division plainly: *"Personality facets determine how a given creature acts, as opposed to personality values which determine what they believe."* Values plus traits dictate **needs** and how often they must be met before focus degrades ([DF wiki: Personality value](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Personality_value), [Personality facet](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Personality_facet)).

**(e)** Transmission is by **inheritance from the civilisation**, not by contact: *"Personal values are also influenced by cultural values via the use of the VALUE entity token"* ([Personality value](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Personality_value)). Individual variation is drawn around the entity's value; there is no in-fortress drift mechanism and no propagation between living dwarves **(no source found for value change over time — treat DF as having *no* cultural drift)**.

**(d)** Contestation is the petition system, and it is priced as an artefact-value demand: dwarves sharing a god or guild petition for a temple/guildhall worth **2000☼** (**6000☼** if Economy is set to Hard at embark), later upgrading to a grand guildhall at **10000☼**. Accepting gives a positive thought, building it gives another, denying gives a negative thought that "can make it into memory" and persists ([DF wiki: Petition](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Petition), [Steam discussion on repeated denials](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/5828254465017207401/)).

### Tropico 3–7 — a faction layer that failed for a decade, and the sequel that redesigns it into a window

**(b) Standing already gates policies, at a published threshold.** T3/T4 faction relations run **0–100**, and several edicts are hard-gated: Martial Law "Requires: Militarists faction relations of **60+**", as do Inquisition, Papal Visit and Amnesty ([Edicts T3 / Domestic Policy](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_3)/Domestic_Policy)). Standing deltas are small integers: Military Coup **+20** Militarists, Velvet Revolution **+20** Intellectuals / **−15** China, Heir Apparent **+10 with all** ([Rise To Power](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Rise_To_Power_(Tropico_3_and_4))).

**(a/e) T6 membership model — the part that broke.** A citizen holds one slot on each of **4 opposed axes** (Capitalist–Communist, Religious–Militarist, Environmentalist–Industrialist, Intellectual–Conservative) and their approval bonus is **averaged across those ≤4 memberships**; the baseline split is normal-distributed and symmetric, "with most members being moderate, a smaller amount being strong and an even smaller amount being die-hard". Media buildings give a flat **25% chance to shift orientation** per rest/work cycle and **cannot affect die-hards**, who also sway undecided family members ([Guide to political support](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1702878108)).

**(f) Card eradication already exists here, priced and imprinting.** T3 `Outlaw Faction` costs **$15,000** and "every supporter leaves and **up to half of them may become rebels**"; T3 `Book BBQ` makes "the Intellectuals faction instantly lose **50%** of its supporters and the chance for immigrants with this leaning is **halved**" — suppression of both the stock *and* the inflow ([Megalomania](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_3)/Megalomania), [Domestic Policy](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_3)/Domestic_Policy)). T6 `Martial Law` costs **$7,500** for Militarist **+30 / all others −15**, liberty **−35**, rebel chance **+15%**, and **−15 personal experience for every eligible voter**; `Nuclear Testing` is free, pays **+$100,000 once**, and costs **−15 with every faction** ([Edicts T6](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_6))).

**(d) T5's rebel model is the only fully datamined one, and it separates two questions cleanly.** A colony-level Rebellion Threat sets scale and frequency — **L2 Low: 14 rebels, 0 leaders, 15% chance; L3 Medium: 20/1/30%; L4 High: 26/2/45%; L5 Very High: 33/4/60% plus palace attacks** — recounted every **2 months**, with an attack rolled only if ≥12 months have passed since the last one. *Who* rebels is a separate per-citizen query: age 18–50, not in a military building, **lowest happiness chosen with Liberty counted twice**, and explicitly "**Approval has nothing to do with this elevation**". Threat deltas include successful attack **+1**, failed attack **−1 at 33% chance**, **killing a non-rebel by order +1**, treasury over **$100k +1**, and **elections denied +1 / elections cheated +1** ([Rebel Mechanics T5](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Rebel_Mechanics_(Tropico_5))).

**Failures — the best-documented faction failure in the sample.** (1) *Standing is decoupled from approval, so factions are ignorable*: "**approval has nothing to do with how happy factions are directly**", and pleasing capitalists does not anger communists ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/1814296907961894389/)). The community guide states the endpoint plainly — "it's entirely possible to **have all factions hate your guts while you maintain a 70% approval rating**" ([guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1702878108)). (2) *Faction power is decoupled from faction size*: 20 environmentalists veto 50+ industrial buildings, and players report **ultimatums from factions with zero members** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/1/2952595757899259460/)). (3) *Demands are randomised noise*: "The military guy is constantly asking for roller coasters… **Roller. Coasters.**", and "your religious zealots are arguing against your communists… **even when they both want the same identical outcome**" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/1814296907960245120/), [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/3110267314137550740/)). (4) *Everything is trivially swung or bypassed*: reputation moved "**from 5% to 60**" in one pre-election turn ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/1771511442694405278)); the guide teaches "quick build a few buildings that a faction likes just before the election. **You can demolish them straight after**" and "**Just reduce their membership using propaganda until they don't matter**"; and the Broker's `Stage A Distraction` at **$2,500** simply "dodges the goal" of any ultimatum ([guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1702878108), [Ultimatums](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatums)). PC Gamer's review lands it: citizens "can protest and even rebel, but… **they're too easy to placate** — even when they don't have a roof over their head" ([PC Gamer, 79/100](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/pcgamer/2465065785300982905)). Tropico 5 failed the opposite way — one law solved elections: "My approval rating was excellent, though, **because only the rich could vote**" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/tropico-5-review)).

**Nothing was ever reworked.** Across **23 updates and hotfixes (Mar 2019 → Apr 2025)** the T6 changelog holds only bug fixes, added demands and single-edict numbers — no systemic faction or approval rebalance — and T5's v1.02–v1.11 contain **zero** faction/rebel/approval balance lines. Update 18 (2022-09-06) instead shipped Creative Mode toggles for **Disable elections / Disable rebels / Disable demands / Disable invasions**. Kalypso's pre-launch promise had been the opposite: approval juggling would be "**more nuanced**… reserved only for the most skilled and righteous of Presidentes" ([T6 FAQ](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-6-faq-1)).

**(b) Tropico 7 (2026) redesigns it into exactly our mechanic.** Each citizen now aligns with **one** faction (reversing the 4-way averaging), 8 factions sit on a 2-axis **Political Matrix**, and — the key line — **"Available edicts depend on where El Presidente is located in the Political Matrix"**, elaborated as *"you cannot enact a very left-wing law if you have previously chosen a very right-wing constitution."* The override is earned, not bought: *"If you fulfil the demands of political factions, you can receive a 'faction deal'… to activate an edict that falls within a faction's sphere but lies outside your political orientation."* Random ultimatums are deleted — *"**No more random ultimatums popping up like bad weather**"* — and citizens carry **10 explicit satisfaction scores** feeding the vote, with the matrix stated as making politics *"more transparent"* ([T7 FAQ](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-7-faq-1), [Dev-Log #1](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1830163047259921), [Dev-Log #2](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-7-dev-log-2)). A studio that lived with this failure for seven years is shipping our core loop as the fix.

### Sid Meier's Alpha Centauri — the cleanest multi-axis ideology with hard prohibitions

**(a)** Four categories, one active model each: Politics, Economics, Values, Future Society. Each model is a vector of effects on eleven ratings (Economy, Efficiency, Support, Talent, Morale, Police, Growth, Planet, Probe, Industry, Research) in roughly −5..+5: e.g. Police State = +2 Support, +2 Police, −2 Efficiency; Free Market = +2 Economy, −3 Planet, **−5 Police**; Thought Control = +2 Morale, +2 Police, +2 Probe, −3 Support ([Alpha Centauri wiki: Social Engineering](https://alphacentauri2.info/wiki/Social_Engineering), [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Sid_Meier's_Alpha_Centauri/Social_Engineering)).

**(b) Hard ideological prohibition.** Every faction has a *preference* and an *aversion*, and **a faction may not select its aversion at all**, human-controlled or not ([Alpha Centauri wiki](https://alphacentauri2.info/wiki/Social_Engineering), [Miraheze wiki](https://alphacentauri.miraheze.org/wiki/Social_Engineering)). This is the closest shipped thing to a hard window edge — and note it is a *per-faction identity* constraint, not a shifting societal one.

**(f) Radical change is superlinearly priced.** Switching costs energy credits by *number of simultaneous changes*, not by distance: on Thinker difficulty 1/2/3/4 changes cost **32 / 108 / 256 / 500** — the 8·n³ curve ([Alpha Centauri wiki](https://alphacentauri2.info/wiki/Social_Engineering)). Choosing a model another faction is averse to also makes them "more antagonistic" diplomatically.

### Old World (Mohawk Games / Soren Johnson) — legitimacy as an action-economy multiplier

**(c)** Legitimacy rises from completing Ambitions, finishing Wonders and earning epithets ("the Wise", "the Peacemaker"), and it directly sets **how many Orders (actions) the ruler gets each turn** — legitimacy is not a permission gate, it is throughput ([Mohawk: Introducing Old World](https://mohawkgames.com/2020/04/14/old-world-first-post/), [pcgamesn review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/old-world/review)). Families are standing stakeholders with their own opinion, so every law sits inside a patronage network rather than a popularity number.

### Norland (Long Jaunt / Hooded Horse) — belief as a mob trigger with a named threshold

**(d)** Religious fanatics rebel if **average peasant mood over a day falls below 25**; the game first offers an out — sacrifice a lord — and otherwise the fanatics form an army, burn the Library and kill "anyone who is insufficiently devout". A bishop with a mortal enemy among the city's lords can trigger the same uprising independently of mood ([Magic Game World: managing loyalty, rebellions and terror](https://www.magicgameworld.com/norland-managing-loyalty-rebellions-and-terror/), [Norland official wiki: Population](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Norland/Population)). Useful shape: a *named scapegoat option* offered before the riot fires.

## 2. Patterns that recur

1. **Nobody ships a hard window; everyone ships a price curve.** Democracy 4 lets you pass anything with enough political capital; SMAC prices *simultaneity* on an 8·n³ curve (32/108/256/500 for 1–4 changes); CK3 charges +50% and +25% time to *replace* a tradition versus fill an empty slot; Frostpunk 1 charges irreversibility. The only hard prohibition in the sample is SMAC's faction aversion — and that is a fixed identity trait, not a moving societal boundary.
2. **Two clocks per policy: instant reaction, slow normalisation.** Democracy 4 splits these explicitly (conservatives angry today, national liberalism up over years). Frostpunk 2 splits them as trust/fervour hits (instant) versus zeitgeist movement (slow). Any single "approval" number collapses the design.
3. **Equilibrium decay beats hard caps.** CK3 acceptance decays −0.01/year per point above a *statically computed baseline*, so drift is a pull toward a structural equilibrium rather than a clamp. Stellaris frames the same idea as "population percentages converge on the attraction percentages".
4. **Legitimacy loss is staged, never instant.** Frostpunk 1: bar critical → ultimatum, move it 15% in 2 days. Frostpunk 2: Despised → ultimatum, 0% → 25%. Norland: mood < 25 → *first* an offer to sacrifice a lord, then the mob. The grace window is where the drama lives.
5. **Legitimacy is a multiplier on throughput, not a permission bit.** Old World converts it to Orders per turn; CK3 to claim cost, alliance acceptance and faction pressure; Democracy 4 to political capital per turn. None of them use it as a simple yes/no gate.
6. **Suppression always has a named second-order price.** Frostpunk 2's Round Up arrests up to 500 and spikes Tension; Stellaris Suppress cuts attraction 75% but drops approval 50% and drives emigration; SMAC's Police rating gates nerve stapling. The cost is always paid in a *different currency* than the benefit.
7. **Radicalisation is a pipeline with named stages, not a threshold.** Democracy 4: angry voter → pressure group → radicalised member → terrorist organisation. Frostpunk 2: neglected community → faction → splinter faction (Frostlanders → Pilgrims) → fervour → riot.
8. **Petitions are cheap, legible contestation.** Dwarf Fortress prices them as an artefact value: a guildhall/temple petition demands a meeting place worth **2000☼** (**6000☼** on Hard economy), a grand guildhall **10000☼**; accepting gives a positive thought, denying a negative one that "can make it into memory" ([DF wiki: Petition](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Petition)).

## 3. Failure modes to design against

1. **The authoritarian ramp becomes a dominant strategy and the game answers only in prose.** In Frostpunk 1 you can escalate control laws until "hope will never be a problem again"; the sole penalty is a judgemental epilogue ([review roundup](https://www.newgamenetwork.com/article/1892/frostpunk-review/), [Imbierowicz, DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2025/2024/2021)). If harshness monotonically reduces the pressure that would punish harshness, the loop is degenerate.
2. **Scripted revolt teaches players their input is noise.** Frostpunk 2 players report "in the final act, one of them will act up… whether you have kept them happy or not" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1601580/discussions/0/583876502953702073)). If contestation fires on a timer, it stops being a signal.
3. **A pushback layer with no downside case is ignorable.** Stellaris factions only ever *add* Unity, so an angry faction costs nothing but forgone income; Paradox closed the counter-argument **As Designed** ([report](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/stellaris-faction-unity-gain-is-messed-up-bad-v3-5-1-73bb.1543571/)).
4. **Democratising the sim can paralyse it.** Reviewers of Frostpunk 2: the more you appease factions, the more the government seizes up and laws get weaponised by opponents ([pcgamesn](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/review)). A veto-rich council plus a 10-week vote cadence turns politics into an errand queue.
5. **Per-carrier ideology is a performance trap.** Stellaris keyed pop groups on ethics in 4.0, the group count exploded, and 4.3 replaced per-pop ethics with *percentages per group* ([DD #370](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-370-4-0-changes-part-4.1728047/), [DD #411](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-411-our-next-4-3-experiment.1904297/)). Design the carrier representation for the population size you actually intend to simulate.
6. **Modifier lists rot into filler.** CK3 1.13 "rebalanced 40+ Cultural Traditions… removed some inconsequential modifiers" ([Patch 1.13](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Patch_1.13)). Every card must change a decision, not a number.
7. **A legitimacy stat with one reliable source becomes a chore.** CK3 legitimacy is farmed via activity spam, loses are asymmetric, and the most popular mod deletes the system ([forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/legitimacy-is-badly-implemented-how-do-we-fix-it.1673331/)).
8. **Aggregate bars erase the people they represent.** The standing complaint about Frostpunk 1 is that citizens reduce to two bars; the complaint about Frostpunk 2 is the opposite failure — politically rich, but "their every day people don't feel as real" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1601580/discussions/0/583876502953702073)). Both directions are reachable.
9. **Hidden numbers block mastery.** Frostpunk 1 never exposes per-law hope/discontent deltas; players resorted to file-diving ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)). For a zero-player sim where watching *is* the game, opacity is worse than in a normal builder.
10. **Decoupled numbers let the whole layer be ignored — or make it fire for no reason.** Tropico keeps faction standing, citizen approval and happiness as three loosely-coupled values, so players find "approval has nothing to do with how happy factions are directly" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/1814296907961894389/)) while Tropico 5 players get "endless rebellions" at 70+ happiness because rebels key off *standing* instead ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/245620/discussions/0/540742667429638096/)). One causal chain, or the player learns to watch the wrong bar.
11. **Randomly-generated demands destroy legibility instantly.** Tropico's militarist faction asking for roller coasters, and factions arguing over a constitution "even when they both want the same identical outcome", is the canonical example ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/0/3110267314137550740/)). Every demand must be derivable from something the watcher can already see on the objector.
12. **When a political layer cannot be fixed, studios ship a switch to turn it off.** Tropico 6's Update 18 added Creative Mode toggles for *Disable elections / Disable rebels / Disable demands / Disable invasions* after 23 updates with no systemic faction rebalance. That toggle is the terminal diagnosis for this class of system.
13. **One cheap bypass voids the entire layer.** Tropico 6 lets the Broker "Stage A Distraction" for **$2,500**, which simply "dodges the goal" of any ultimatum, and the community guide teaches building faction-pleasing structures before an election and demolishing them straight after, plus "reduce their membership using propaganda until they don't matter" ([Ultimatums](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatums), [guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1702878108)). Concessions must be verified against colony state at resolution time, not at promise time.

## 4. Concrete proposals for our design

Each item: **source mechanic → our mapping → what it conflicts with.**

1. **Window as a computed interval, not a designer constant.** *Source:* Democracy 4's "overton splat" plus `Final = Global*Impressionability + Innate*(1-Impressionability)` with Impressionability clamped to **15%–85%** ([Cliffski](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/03/03/improvements-to-the-political-compass-voters-opinions/)). *Ours:* per axis, derive the window edges from percentiles of the live citizen distribution (e.g. 15th–85th percentile of citizen positions on harsh–lenient). The clamp is what guarantees the colony never becomes monolithic, which is what keeps contestation alive after generations. *Conflicts with:* nothing in our spec — it replaces a magic number.
2. **Three bands, not a binary gate.** *Source:* Frostpunk 2 needs **51/100** delegates for a normal law but **67/100** for *Rule* laws that increase the Steward's own power ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-2-council-voting-explained-how-to-pass-every-vote-negotiate-more/)). *Ours:* inside window = ordinary vote; fringe band (outer ~15% of the window on the relevant axis) = passes only with a supermajority *or* a legitimacy expenditure, and always draws contestation; outside = refused outright with protest. *Conflicts with:* the spec's flat "outside → refused". Every shipped game prices rather than forbids; keeping a hard outer edge is fine, but it needs the fringe band in front of it or the mayor's only legible move is to do nothing.
3. **Two clocks on every policy.** *Source:* Democracy 4 separates instant voter anger from slow global-value drift ([Cliffski](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/06/06/liberalism-socialism-tweaks/)). *Ours:* each policy carries `shock` (immediate mood/legitimacy delta scaled by each citizen's axis distance from the policy) and `normalisation` (a small per-season pull of the window edge toward the policy's position, applied **only while the policy remains in force**). *Conflicts with:* nothing — but see 4.
4. **Cynicism to close the enact-repeal exploit.** *Source:* Democracy 4's Cynicism rises from flip-flopping, pre-election bribes and post-election pain, and decays only by being forgotten ([Democracy wiki](https://democracygame.fandom.com/wiki/Cynicism)). *Ours:* repealing a policy within N seasons of enacting it raises colony Cynicism, which caps legitimacy *gain* (not level) until it ages out. Without this, proposal 3 turns "enact at the edge, repeal, repeat" into a free window-shifting engine.
5. **Anti-ratchet decay on the window; put the slowness in the cards.** *Source:* CK3 acceptance decays **−0.01/year for every point above a structurally computed baseline** ([CK3 wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Culture)). *Ours:* compute a *baseline* window from the tradition-card carrier distribution, and let the live window decay toward that baseline each season. Policies move the live window; only cards move the baseline. *Conflicts with:* "eugenics reachable after generations" if decay is applied naively — resolve it exactly this way: card composition is the generational clock, the live window is the tactical one.
6. **Entertainers and schools as multipliers, not flat adds.** *Source:* Stellaris governing-ethics-attraction is multiplicative and its edicts are large — Hearts and Minds **+100%**, One Vision **+50%**, Conformists **+30%**, and a fanatic state ethic is **×3** vs moderate **×2** ([Stellaris wiki](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Ethics)). *Ours:* an entertainer pushing a narrative multiplies the drift rate of one axis for citizens in earshot; a school multiplies transmission-at-birth for cards taught there. Multipliers compose without needing balance passes per building.
7. **Legitimacy does two jobs, so it needs three sources.** *Source:* Old World turns Legitimacy into **Orders per turn** ([Mohawk](https://mohawkgames.com/2020/04/14/old-world-first-post/)); CK3's legitimacy is the most-disliked of its three culture systems precisely because activity spam is its only reliable source ([forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/legitimacy-is-badly-implemented-how-do-we-fix-it.1673331/)). *Ours:* legitimacy multiplies the mayor's vote weight *and* sets the fringe-band width — which is exactly the concentration CK3 got punished for. Give it at least three independent, non-spammable inflows: surviving a named crisis, fulfilling a granted petition, and delivering a promise made to win a vote.
8. **Never depose instantly — issue an ultimatum with a named remedy.** *Source:* Frostpunk 1 gives a **15% in 2 days** ultimatum at critical Hope/Discontent ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c)); Frostpunk 2 gives **0% → 25% Trust** ([pcgamesn](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/trust)); Norland offers to **sacrifice a lord** before the fanatics march at mood < 25 ([Magic Game World](https://www.magicgameworld.com/norland-managing-loyalty-rebellions-and-terror/)). *Ours:* legitimacy 0 opens a Grace season with a concrete demand and an explicit scapegoat option (dismiss an official, repeal a named policy, hand over a card's leading carrier). For a zero-player sim this is the moment the watcher most wants to intervene — put the indirect influence here.
9. **Promises as the currency for fringe policies.** *Source:* Frostpunk 2 draws **3 random demands** from a faction and the player picks **1**; breaking it "worsens relations significantly", while **Pressure** always adds votes but damages relations with *every* faction ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/council/zf115fb)). *Ours:* to pass a fringe policy the mayor picks 1 of 3 concessions drawn from the objecting citizens' *cards* — the concession is legible because it comes from a card the watcher can already see. *Conflicts with:* FP2's 10-week vote cadence, which reviewers call an errand queue ([pcgamesn](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/review)); tie the promise deadline to the same season the policy takes effect, so it resolves rather than accumulates.
10. **Contestation as a named pipeline with per-stage de-escalation.** *Source:* Democracy 4 escalates angry voter → pressure group → radicalised member → terrorist organisation ([guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/8590-democracy-4.html)); Frostpunk 2 splinters neglected communities into more extreme factions (Frostlanders → Pilgrims) ([essay](https://actpotential.substack.com/p/frostpunk-2-is-a-video-game-about)). *Ours:* petition → work-to-rule → quiet sabotage → council override → exile. Each stage exposes one de-escalation action that costs a *different* resource than the one under pressure (grant the petition = build cost; buy off = food; suppress = legitimacy).
11. **Petitions priced as a concrete demand, denial imprints memory.** *Source:* Dwarf Fortress guild/temple petitions demand a room worth **2000☼** (**6000☼** on Hard economy, **10000☼** for a grand guildhall); accepting gives a positive thought, denying a negative one that "can make it into memory" ([DF wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Petition)). *Ours:* every petition names a buildable or enactable thing with a visible cost; denial imprints a card on the petitioners. This is our cheapest source of card *births* and needs no event authoring.
12. **Price eradication in a second currency and make it imprint.** *Source:* Frostpunk 2's Round Up arrests **up to 500** faction members over weeks, cutting Fervour but causing "a large increase in Tension" ([sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/how-deradicalize-factions-frostpunk-2)); Stellaris Suppress cuts attraction **−75%** but costs **−50%** approval and drives emigration ([Stellaris wiki](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Factions)). *Ours:* eradicating a card strips it from carriers *and* stamps "Fear of Authority" on every witness. Make that card widen the harsh edge of the window while **reducing all legitimacy gain** — so harshness buys itself more room and less standing. That asymmetry is the direct fix for the Frostpunk 1 failure where escalation monotonically removes the pressure that would punish escalation.
13. **Give dying cards a re-concentration term, then cap it.** *Source:* CK3 fervor below **40%** triggers a heresy that converts **10–25 counties**, and every defecting ruler returns **+15% fervor** to the religion they left ([CK3 wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Faith)). *Ours:* when a card's carrier count drops below a threshold, surviving carriers get a bounded transmission bonus — persecution concentrates the faithful. *Conflicts with:* "dies with its last carrier" only if the bonus is uncapped; cap it so extinction stays reachable but never cheap.
14. **Aggregate the carriers, don't iterate them.** *Source:* Stellaris keyed pop groups on ethics in 4.0, the group count exploded, and 4.3 replaced per-pop ethics with percentages per group ([DD #411](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-411-our-next-4-3-experiment.1904297/)). *Ours:* keep cards as bitsets on citizens (that is the fiction), but maintain colony-level counts and per-axis means updated on birth/death/transmission events, not scanned per tick. Every window, vote and drift query reads the aggregate.
15. **Gate eugenics on card composition, not on a timer or a hard ban.** *Source:* SMAC's faction *aversion* is a genuine hard prohibition, but it is a fixed identity trait, not a shifting boundary ([Alpha Centauri wiki](https://alphacentauri2.info/wiki/Social_Engineering)). *Ours:* the policy becomes *proposable* only when a prerequisite card (e.g. "Culled the Weak Winter") exceeds a carrier fraction **and** the harsh edge of the window has crossed a threshold. That makes "generations away" a state condition the watcher can read off the UI rather than an invisible countdown.
16. **Expose every number.** *Source:* Frostpunk 1 hides per-law Hope/Discontent deltas, and players resorted to file-diving to find them ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/2266943550311885540/)). *Ours:* in a zero-player sim the readout *is* the game. Axis positions, window edges, per-policy shock vs normalisation, card carrier counts and the exact vote tally all belong on screen — ASCII makes this cheap.
17. **Position gates the policy set; a promise buys one exception.** *Source:* Tropico 7 — *"Available edicts depend on where El Presidente is located in the Political Matrix"*, *"you cannot enact a very left-wing law if you have previously chosen a very right-wing constitution"*, with a **faction deal** earned by fulfilling demands as the only override ([T7 Dev-Log #1](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1830163047259921), [T7 FAQ](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-7-faq-1)). *Ours:* this is our design, arrived at independently by a studio that spent seven years with the failure it fixes — adopt it wholesale, including the rule that the override is *earned by service to the objectors*, never bought with a generic currency. *Conflicts with:* nothing; it also supersedes any temptation to let legitimacy alone buy an out-of-window policy, which would collapse proposal 7 back into "spend points, ignore the culture".
18. **One position per citizen; cards modify it, they are not averaged into it.** *Source:* Tropico 6 let a citizen belong to **4 factions** and averaged their approval, producing ultimatums from factions with zero members and standing decoupled from size; Tropico 7 reverts to *"each citizen aligns with one faction, making their demands and position easier to understand"* ([T7 FAQ](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-7-faq-1), [Steam on zero-member ultimatums](https://steamcommunity.com/app/492720/discussions/1/2952595757899259460/)). *Ours:* a citizen has exactly one vector on the three axes; tradition cards *shift* that vector and supply the vocabulary for their objections, but a citizen is never "a member of" several cards' blocs whose demands get averaged. *Conflicts with:* nothing in the card model — cards stay a set; only the *political position* stays singular.
19. **Delete random ultimatums before writing one.** *Source:* Tropico 7's stated fix, verbatim: *"No more random ultimatums popping up like bad weather"* ([T7 FAQ](https://www.kalypsomedia.com/post/tropico-7-faq-1)). *Ours:* every escalation step in proposal 10 must name the policy, card, or unmet petition that caused it. *Conflicts with:* "born from events" card generation, if events fire on a timer — gate event-born cards on colony state, not on the clock.
20. **Split "how much unrest" from "who acts on it", and score them on different stats.** *Source:* Tropico 5's datamined model — a colony-level Rebellion Threat sets scale and frequency (**L2: 14 rebels / 15% chance; L3: 20/30%; L4: 26/45%; L5: 33/60%**, recounted every 2 months, attack rolled only if ≥12 months since the last), while *who* rebels is a separate per-citizen query on lowest happiness with **Liberty counted twice** and "**Approval has nothing to do with this elevation**"; threat deltas include **killing a non-rebel by order +1**, **elections denied +1**, **elections cheated +1** ([Rebel Mechanics T5](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Rebel_Mechanics_(Tropico_5))). *Ours:* colony Unrest sets how large and how often contestation events fire and carries a minimum interval so protest cannot spam; the participants are drawn separately from carriers of the offended card. The "kill a non-rebel → +1 threat" term is the cleanest existing precedent for proposal 12 — suppression feeds the very number it is meant to reduce. *Conflicts with:* nothing; it replaces a single blended unrest stat, which is failure mode 10.
21. **Card eradication has a shipped price list — copy its shape.** *Source:* Tropico 3's `Outlaw Faction` costs **$15,000** and "every supporter leaves and **up to half of them may become rebels**"; `Book BBQ` makes the Intellectuals "instantly lose **50%** of its supporters" *and* **halves the immigration chance** for that leaning ([Megalomania](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_3)/Megalomania), [Domestic Policy](https://tropico.fandom.com/wiki/Edicts_(Tropico_3)/Domestic_Policy)). *Ours:* eradicating a card should hit three things at once — the carrier stock, the *transmission rate* (birth and contact) going forward, and a fraction of ex-carriers converting into active contestants. Suppressing the stock without suppressing transmission just buys a few seasons. *Conflicts with:* proposal 13's re-concentration bonus — resolve by applying the transmission penalty as a multiplier *after* the small-population bonus, so persecution still concentrates but cannot outrun a sustained campaign.
