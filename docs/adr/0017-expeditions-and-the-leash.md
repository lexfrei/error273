# 0017. Expeditions, the leash, and dying out of sight

- Status: proposed
- Amended: 2026-08-26
- Date: 2026-08-25

## Context

With no map edge a hauler can walk until the cold takes them, and the colony needs a reason to come back, a real chance of not coming back, and caution — none of which may be a scripted return order, because the constants already price the whole question.

## Decision

The leash is the warmth need itself: one an hour lost outside the fire's reach and two an hour regained inside it, from a ceiling of a hundred to a death at zero, which makes an out-and-back excursion cost twice its depth and puts the colony's reach at fifty cells past the freezing line. Caution is the lookahead a fixed threshold does not have, so warmth's acting mark becomes the cost of getting home plus a margin under ADR 0003, with the margin a universal constant shifted only by the tradition cards a citizen carries, and a patch is worked down further the farther it is because travel counted as dead time is what sets the give-up point. Discovery is a deliberate scout drawn from citizens the ballot's survival tier has nothing to say about, walking a heavy-tailed step distribution drawn from seeded noise. A waystation is the fourth building on the ballot, elected by the same detour hours that already elect a boiler and sited where those detours concentrate, running on a fuel line into a small on-site cache so that a chain of them is a standing tax on the workforce that grows with its length.

## Consequences

A citizen who does not return is recorded as missing rather than dead: the colony learns only that they did not come back, and closing the event costs either a second citizen the same walk to recover the body or the build capacity to raise a memorial without one. Sending a hauler further is right for a colony short of stores and wrong for a citizen short of warmth, so the two budgets disagree by construction and the citizen who dies is the one a hungry colony sent on a thin day; the expedition layer is therefore only worth its weight if the risk and reward gradient of ADR 0016 makes a distant trip beat staying home, which the balance log has to show.

## Amendment, 2026-08-25, on what was built

Three things landed differently from the way this ADR wrote them down, and one thing it did not mention at all.

**The colony works only ground somebody has stood in.** This is not in the decision above and it is what makes a scout's walk worth a citizen's day. With the search drawing squares on demand out to its limit, a hauler already reached any patch inside it and discovery bought nothing; option A's circularity only bites if hauling is confined to what has been walked. Knowing a square and holding one are separate: memory is dropped when nobody is near and drawn again from the seed, and the colony does not forget where it has been. A founding party knows the squares its near ground touches and nothing further.

**A chain grows a link at a time.** The ADR says a waystation runs on a fuel line and that a chain is a standing tax. It does not say where one may go, and the answer turned out to be forced: a post further out than a hauler can reach and return from is a post nobody can stock, so the site is drawn back along the line until it is one that can be fed. A shed short of wood claims one hauler, the one nearest it. Claiming every hauler was tried and killed the colony in its forty-fourth year -- that is conscription rather than a tax, and the difference is the whole of what makes a chain payable.

**The leash brings a scout home and nothing else had to.** That part worked exactly as written, and is the clearest thing this layer produced: no rule about expeditions was needed, because warmth's marks already rise with distance.

**What the layer does not yet do is buy any reach.** Everything above is built and measured, and known ground still stops at the nine squares the leash alone reaches. A brazier holds a ring of about five cells, so a chain is a row of pinpricks with cold ground between the links, and a citizen staging from the last post has spent most of a walk getting there. Closing that needs either a hotter brazier or links set by the ring rather than by what a hauler can survive, and both are numbers that should fall out of what a far haul is worth -- which is the marginal-value layer's arithmetic, not this one's. The two open questions this ADR leaves are therefore inherited rather than answered: the nine squares, and the marginal value that would pay for passing them.

## Amendment, 2026-08-26: the reach was derived, and a corridor cannot pay for itself

This decision deferred two numbers -- what a waystation puts out and how far apart the links of a chain stand -- to be derived once far ground was worth working. Far ground is now worth working: a trip's take depends on the ground, a hauler stays at a patch while the next hour beats the trip's average, and the choice of patch is what it is worth an hour rather than what is nearest. The derivation was done and it does not produce a number. It produces a finding, and the arithmetic behind it is written out as `links_to`, `corridor_cost`, `heat_for_step` and `corridor_break_even` beside the tests that read them, rather than in this paragraph, so that it is re-run on every gate and fails the moment a constant it rests on moves. It decides nothing in the colony and no system calls it: a derivation whose answer is do-not-build-this is a claim about the constants, and it belongs where the constants are checked.

**The two numbers as they stand do not describe a chain.** A post is sited up to a step from the warmth that feeds it, and a step is thirty-seven and a half cells; a post's own disc is four and a half. So what the colony builds is not a corridor but a string of separate braziers with twenty-eight cells of open cold between each pair, and a hauler crossing between them is as unwarmed as if none of them were lit. Closing that gap by output alone would want a brazier putting out more than eighty-two against the generator's sixty-six -- a brazier that outshines the hearth is not a brazier, it is a second hearth, and the decision that wanted one is not this one.

**Closing it would not help, because the corridor cannot pay for itself at any output.** What a lit chain costs is counted in wood a day and what it buys is counted in what one hauler gains a day by making the same trip warm rather than cold; the ratio is a number of haulers who must be working past the last link for the thing to break even. At the output the colony builds today that number is **ninety-six haulers for a corridor thirty cells long and two hundred and ninety-four for one reaching the far edge**. The colony is founded with thirty people and has never in any measurement held more than fifty-six. Raising the output shortens the chain, and in this model a post burns the same whatever it puts out, which is as generous to corridors as the arithmetic can be made -- and the best corner of that generosity still wants seventeen haulers permanently past the last link, on ground the flow bands say the colony never has more than a handful working at once.

**The mechanism is legible and it is not the reach.** A post burns whether anybody is out there or not, at four wood a day, while a hauler's gain from a warm trip is a fraction of a unit a day -- because a far trip is already several times worse an hour than the near ring, and taking the cold out of it improves the cost by about a third of a term. A fixed standing charge against a small per-user gain does not come out, and no reach makes it. What would change it is a post whose cost falls when nobody uses it, or far ground worth several times more than it is worth now; both are other layers' business.

**Nothing was tuned.** The two numbers are left exactly where they were, because moving them cannot make a corridor pay and would only put a changed constant in the log with nothing behind it. Three tests hold the finding: that the current step is wider than twice the current reach, that a corridor does not pay at the shipping output at any distance, and that a hotter brazier does not rescue it.
