# 0009. Lifecycle: frailty instead of a death age, and inherited trades

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-08-25
- Amended: 2026-08-25

## Context

Generational play needs births and deaths that do not arrive in cohorts. Gating births on a binary condition and killing at a hard age puts two step functions in series, which produces a baby boom and then a synchronised die-off a lifetime later; the genre's own players describe the result as an unrecoverable spiral. The colony's growth loop already carries the first half of that shape, since a birth requires a free bed.

## Decision

There is no death at a fixed age: hardiness decays with age from around 40 and each cold snap rolls against current hardiness. Expected lifespan is an output of the childhood formation of ADR 0015 and of the acclimatisation term that rides on it, with the roll of about a tenth either side surviving as the unexplained residual on top rather than as the thing that decides a citizen. Aging runs one year per calendar year, so the date on screen never contradicts a citizen's age. Births are a per-couple seasonal probability scaled by spare housing rather than a hard free-bed gate, and warmth and food surplus modulate the maturation rate, so the age pyramid is an output of colony health. Competence is stored as per-citizen experience per work type, while the household supplies the inheritance rule: a child takes the household trade by default with a fraction of the head's experience in it, and an unassigned laborer pool of a fifth to a quarter of the workforce absorbs whatever no household's trade covers.

## Consequences

Winter becomes the agent of death, which is what the setting wants, and deaths spread out instead of arriving together. The age pyramid compounds rather than merely reporting maturation, because a colony that fed its children well is later a colony of citizens who outlast their winters. A trade can die with a household, so scarcity becomes a demographic fact the watcher can see coming rather than a random shortage.
